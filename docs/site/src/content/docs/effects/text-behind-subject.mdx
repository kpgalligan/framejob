Text Behind Subject puts a title behind the person in a camera clip or photo. The person stands in front of the title, and the room stays behind it. A shape, picture or graphic can go behind a person the same way.

  menu="Insert ▸ Text Behind Subject"
  where="Clip tab ▸ Picture ▸ Behind Subject"
  tools={['text_behind_subject', 'set_video']}
  needs="Nothing. The masks are made on your Mac, with no key and no upload."

## Text behind subject in one step

<Steps>

1. Move the playhead to where the title should start, over the camera clip or photo.

2. If several clips play there, select the one with the person.

3. Choose **Insert ▸ Text Behind Subject**. It has no keyboard shortcut.

4. The new title is selected, so type your text in the Clip tab's [**Text** sub-tab](/text-and-shapes/text-clips/).

</Steps>

One **Undo** takes it all back. The step is named **Text Behind Subject**.

Here's what the command does:

- It adds a track named "Behind Subject" directly above the clip's track, and above any [matte tracks](/canvas/matte-tracks/) in front of it.
- It puts a title on that track, in the [look a new text clip starts with](/text-and-shapes/text-clips/#add-a-text-clip), running from the playhead for 4 seconds, or to the clip's end if that's sooner.
- It puts the title behind the subject.
- If the clip had no [Remove Background](/effects/remove-background/), it turns that on with **Background** set to **Keep** and **Subject** set to **Person**, so the picture stays whole and the person still hides the title. A clip that already has a cutout keeps it.

It works with a clip of a video file or a picture that's playing at the playhead, with its picture on, on a shown track that isn't a matte track. Of those, it takes the front-most selected clip, else the front-most with Remove Background on, else the front-most.

The item is greyed while a [nest](/editing/nests/) is open, and when no such clip plays at the playhead.

## Behind subject by hand

Any clip that draws can go behind a subject, except an adjustment clip or a nest clip.

<Steps>

1. Turn on [Remove Background](/effects/remove-background/) for the camera clip. Set **Background** to **Keep** if you want the room to stay.

2. Put the clip you want behind the person on a track in front of the camera clip, and select it.

3. In the Clip tab's **Picture** sub-tab, open the **Behind Subject** section and tick **Behind subject**.

</Steps>

The section comes right after **Transform** and is closed by default, and its header reads "on" or "off". The box is off by default and shows as mixed over a selection that disagrees. Changing it is one undo step, **Behind Subject**, and **Reset** turns it off.

With one clip selected and the box on, the section says "No cut-out clip is behind it at the playhead." when nothing cuts the clip there.

With an [action](/motion/edit-an-action/) selected, the box sets the action's end, and the clip switches at the action's end. The row shows "at end".

The section isn't offered while a nest is open.

## What cuts the clip

A clip with **Behind subject** on is cut, at every moment, by the subject of every cutout clip on a track behind it. Any clip with Remove Background on counts, with **Background** set to **Remove** or **Keep**.

Track order decides it. A cutout clip on a track in front of the clip doesn't cut it.

Where no mask is drawn yet, or no subject is found, the clip draws whole. See [How the masks are made](/effects/remove-background/#how-the-masks-are-made).

## Good to know

- A subject inside a nest cuts a top-level title when the nest clip is behind the title. A top-level camera never cuts a title inside a nest, because a nest is one picture to the timeline.
- The clip's [shadow](/effects/shadow/) is cut with it.
- A clip cut this way gets no [motion blur](/motion/motion-blur/). It draws sharp, and so does the outline that cuts it.

## With an agent

`text_behind_subject` does what the menu item does, and it can also take the title's text, its start time and the clip to go behind. It works on the top-level timeline only, and it leaves the selection as it was. An agent can also turn `behind_subject` on inside a nest with `set_video`, where the section isn't offered, and it works among the clips of that nest. See [Tools: text, shapes and clip names](/mcp/tools-clip-properties/#text_behind_subject) and [Tools: picture and grade](/mcp/tools-set-video/#behind_subject).
