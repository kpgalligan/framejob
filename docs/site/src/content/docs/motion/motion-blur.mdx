Motion blur streaks a moving clip along the way it moved during each frame, as a camera's shutter would. It takes the hard steps out of a fast zoom, pan or slide.

<AtAGlance
  where="Clip tab ▸ Picture sub-tab ▸ Transform ▸ Motion Blur"
  tools={['set_video', 'set_action_end']}
/>

## Add motion blur

<Steps>

1. Select a clip that moves.

2. On the Clip tab's **Picture** sub-tab, open **Transform**.

3. Raise **Motion Blur** above 0%.

</Steps>

**Motion Blur** runs from 0 to 100%, with quick picks at 0, 25, 50 and 100. It's off by default.

The number is the share of one frame the shutter stays open for. 50% is a film camera's 180° shutter, so it's a good place to start. At 100% the clip streaks along everything it moved in that frame.

The blur shows when the clip moves:

- A zoom or pan in an [action](/motion/actions/).
- A [motion preset](/motion/motion-presets/).
- The [nest](/editing/nests/) it sits in moving.

A clip's [recorded cursor](/recording/cursor-settings/) streaks along its own path too, even when the clip stands still. The cursor's click ripples stay sharp.

The Transform section's **Reset** puts **Motion Blur** back to 0% with the section's other rows.

[Auto Zoom](/recording/auto-zoom/) has a **Motion blur** box of its own. Ticked, it sets 50% on the clips it zooms, unless they already have a blur.

## How it is drawn

The blur is the same in the preview, in [Save Frame](/export/save-frame/), in every export and in a frame an agent renders. Each frame draws the clip 16 times at its positions across the shutter and averages them. The shutter is centred on the frame.

A clip that doesn't move in a frame draws exactly as it does with the blur off, so a clip that moves for only part of its length blurs only while it moves.

## Ramp the blur in and out

**Motion Blur** can change inside an action, so a blur can build up and fall away.

<Steps>

1. [Select the action](/motion/select-an-action/).

2. Set **Motion Blur**. With an action selected, the row sets the value the action ends on.

</Steps>

The blur moves from where it stood when the action began to the new value, along the action's curve. See [Set what an action animates to](/motion/edit-an-action/).

## With blend modes

A clip with a [blend mode](/canvas/blend-modes/) blends its averaged picture once. A screened clip, for example, screens its blurred picture over what's under it.

## Where it stays sharp

These draw sharp whatever **Motion Blur** says, and the row keeps its value:

- A clip in a [transition](/motion/transitions/), for the frames of the overlap, and every clip inside a nest clip that's in one.
- A clip on a [matte track](/canvas/matte-tracks/), and the clips a matte cuts.
- A clip cut by [Text Behind Subject](/effects/text-behind-subject/), and the copy of the person it draws.
- A clip with a [drop shadow](/effects/shadow/).
- A nest clip itself. The blur goes on the clips inside it. See [Nests and adjustment clips](#nests-and-adjustment-clips).

## Nests and adjustment clips

A nest clip can't take motion blur, so the row is greyed for it, and for any selection with a nest clip in it. Set the blur on the clips inside the nest instead. They blur when the nest moves.

An [adjustment clip](/color/adjustment-clips/) has no picture to move. It has no **Picture** sub-tab, so there's no row to set.

## With an agent

An agent sets the blur with `motion_blur` in `set_video`, from 0 to 1. 0 is off and the default, and 0.5 is the row's 50%. `set_action_end` sets the value an action ends on, and `render_frame` draws the blur as an export would.

On a nest clip or an adjustment clip, a value above 0 stops with an error and 0 always goes through. A nest clip's error reads "clip N shows a nest and cannot take motion blur: set it on the clips inside". See [Tools: picture and grade](/mcp/tools-set-video/#motion_blur).
