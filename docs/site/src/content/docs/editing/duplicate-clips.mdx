Duplicate makes copies of clips without going through the clipboard. Press a key to put them right after the originals, or hold ⌥ and drag them where you want them.

## Duplicate the selection

Select one or more clips and press <kbd>⌘D</kbd>. **Edit ▸ Duplicate** and the timeline bar's **Duplicate** button do the same. With nothing selected, the menu item is greyed out and the button beeps.

The button sits in the [timeline bar](/getting-started/editor-window/#timeline-bar) with the other one-click clip tools, which split, trim, ripple delete and add markers, text and shapes.

Each copy goes on the same track as its original, starting right after the selection's span. A selection of several clips keeps its internal timing: if it runs from 10 s to 14 s, every copy starts 4 s later than its original.

The copies are one undo step, called Duplicate.

## Drag a copy

To place copies somewhere else, hold <kbd>⌥</kbd> as you press on a clip, then drag. The copies follow the pointer and the originals stay where they are.

- If the clip you press is part of the selection, the whole selection is copied. If it isn't, only that clip is.
- The copies snap and change tracks as a [move](/editing/move-clips/) does. Drop past the top or bottom of the stack to make a new track for them.
- A spot the timeline can't take is skipped. The copies stay at the last spot along the drag that worked, and if none did, nothing changes.
- The whole drag is one undo step, called Duplicate Clips.

Hold only <kbd>⌥</kbd>. <kbd>⌥⇧</kbd> on a clip is a [slip](/editing/slip-and-roll/), not a copy.

:::caution
An ⌥-drag makes its copies by duplicating first, so it needs the room a <kbd>⌘D</kbd> would need. If Duplicate [wouldn't work](#when-duplicate-wont-work) at that moment, because the next clip is too close to the selection, the drag moves the originals instead, wherever you drop them. There's no beep or message, and the undo step is called Move Clips. Undo it, then [copy and paste](/editing/copy-and-paste/) the clip, which has no such limit.
:::

## What a copy keeps

A copy is the whole clip: its source, trim, speed, name, label, picture and sound settings, and actions. From then on it's a clip of its own, so changing one doesn't change the other.

- **Linked clips.** If a clip has a [detached sound](/audio/detach-and-attach/), its partner is copied too and the two copies are linked to each other. A partner on a locked track isn't copied.
- **Nest clips.** A copy of a nest clip made with Duplicate or ⌥-drag shows the same nest as the original, so an edit inside one shows in both. See [Shared and independent nest copies](/editing/nest-copies/).

## When Duplicate won't work

Copies follow the same [overlap rule](/editing/how-edits-work/#the-overlap-rule) as any other placement, and Frame Job doesn't push later clips out of the way.

- A copy may overlap the clip after it by up to half of the shorter clip. The overlap gets a Cross Dissolve, as any new overlap does. See [Transitions](/motion/transitions/).
- A longer overlap stops the duplicate. Frame Job beeps, nothing changes, and the reason appears at the foot of the inspector. See [When an edit is refused](/getting-started/refused-edits/).
- An adjustment clip can't overlap anything, so a copy of one needs clear space.

To duplicate into a tight edit, open room first or use **Edit ▸ Copy** and **Edit ▸ Paste**. A paste that has no room goes on a new track.

## With an agent

`duplicate` takes the clips to copy and puts the copies straight after them, as <kbd>⌘D</kbd> does. There's no agent version of an ⌥-drag: follow `duplicate` with [`move_clip`](/mcp/tools-move-and-trim/#move_clip) to put the copies somewhere else. Both also work inside a nest. See [Tools: copying and nesting](/mcp/tools-copy-and-nest/#duplicate).
