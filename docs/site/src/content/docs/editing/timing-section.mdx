The Timing section is for when you know the number. Type where a clip starts and ends on the timeline, and read how long it is, without dragging an edge.

## Type In and Out

<Steps>

1. Select one clip.

2. In the [inspector's](/getting-started/inspector/) **Clip** tab, choose the **Clip** sub-tab, the last one. **Timing** is its first section, and it starts shut. Click its header to open it, and it stays open until you shut it. Shut, the header still shows the clip's length.

3. Click **In** or **Out** and type a time. `1:23`, `83.5` and `1:23:04` all work, as they do in the toolbar's time field. See [Inspector fields](/getting-started/inspector-fields/).

4. Press <kbd>Return</kbd> or <kbd>Tab</kbd>.

</Steps>

| Field | What it is | What typing does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **In** | Where the clip starts on the timeline | Trims its front edge. The rest of the clip stays where it is. |
| **Out** | Where the clip ends on the timeline | Trims its back edge. |

Both are positions on the timeline, not positions in the file. A later **In** hides the start of the footage, and an earlier one brings it back. To move the whole clip without trimming it, [drag it](/editing/move-clips/) instead.

Each change is one undo step, named Trim Start or Trim End. The time lands on the project's frame grid.

## How far an edge can go

A number out of range doesn't beep. The edge goes as far as it can, and the field shows where it landed.

| Field | Earliest | Latest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **In** | The start of the timeline, or as far back as the file has footage | One frame before the clip's end |
| **Out** | One frame after the clip's start | As far as the file has footage |

Only a clip that plays a video or audio file has a footage limit. Text, shapes, pictures and graphics have no file length to run out of.

Three things leave the clip as it was:

- If you type a new value but it puts the edge where it already is, Frame Job beeps.
- If the text can't be read as a time, Frame Job beeps and the field goes back to the old value.
- If the new edge would break the [overlap rule](/getting-started/refused-edits/), Frame Job beeps and gives the reason at the foot of the inspector.

An overlap the rule allows goes through, and the clip that now overlaps the one before it gets a Cross Dissolve.

## Read the duration

**Duration** shows the clip's length, in the project's [timecode style](/projects/background-and-timecode/). You can't type in it: change the length through **In** and **Out**.

## With several clips selected

**In**, **Out** and **Duration** each describe one clip, so they go away when you select more than one.

If every selected clip plays a video or audio file, Timing stays and holds only the [Reverse](/speed/reverse/) rows, which apply to all of them as one undo step. Its header shows the lengths of the selected clips added together, followed by "total".

If the selection also holds anything else, such as text, a shape, a picture or a freeze frame, there's no Timing section.

## Good to know

- With one clip selected, the Reverse rows appear under **Duration** only for a clip that plays a video or audio file.
- Typing an edge also moves the same edge of a [linked clip](/audio/detach-and-attach/) when the two line up and its track isn't locked. That holds even when you type on the sound half. To move a sound's edge without its picture, [use a drag, <kbd>W</kbd> or <kbd>E</kbd>](/editing/slip-and-roll/#move-a-sound-clips-edge-alone).
- A locked track stops drags, not these fields. If a clip on a locked track is selected, typing still trims it.
- Inside a [nest](/editing/nests/), the times count from the nest's own start.
- [Actions](/motion/how-edits-move-actions/) keep their place measured from the clip's start, so trimming the front edge moves them along the timeline with it.

## With an agent

[`trim_start`](/mcp/tools-move-and-trim/#trim_start) and [`trim_end`](/mcp/tools-move-and-trim/#trim_end) move the same two edges to a time on the timeline, and stop at the same limits. With `alone`, the edge moves without its linked partner's. See [Tools: moving and trimming](/mcp/tools-move-and-trim/).
