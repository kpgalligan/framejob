## Shape a clip's tone

<Steps>

1. Select the clip and open the **Sound** sub-tab of the Clip tab. The **EQ** section starts closed, so open it.

2. For a voice, press a [preset](#presets). Otherwise tick the [part](#the-seven-parts) you want.

3. Set its [Frequency, and its Gain, Q or Slope](#frequency-gain-q-and-slope). The [curve](#the-response-curve) at the top of the section shows the result.

4. Tick [**Bypass**](#bypass) to compare with and without.

</Steps>

The section's header shows how many parts are on, such as "2 on", or "bypassed", or "off" when no part is on, bypassed or not. **Reset** appears there once the EQ has settings, and turns the whole EQ off.

## Presets

Each preset sets one part and turns Bypass off. It leaves the other parts alone, so the three combine.

| Preset | What it sets |
| --- | --- |
| **Rumble Cut** | High-pass on at 80 Hz, 24 dB/oct |
| **De-mud** | Band 1 at 300 Hz, −3 dB, Q 1 |
| **Presence** | Band 3 at 4 kHz, +3 dB, Q 1 |

A preset replaces whatever its part held. Press **De-mud** after setting up Band 1 by hand and your Band 1 settings are gone.

## The seven parts

The EQ has seven parts, each with its own checkbox, and the sound passes through them in this order. Ticking a part turns it on at its default. Unticking it clears that part's settings and leaves the others as they are, so ticking it again starts from the default. To switch parts off without losing them, use [Bypass](#bypass).

A shelf or band starts at 0 dB, and a Gain of 0 dB changes nothing, so you won't hear one until you move its **Gain**. The High-pass and Low-pass take effect as soon as you tick them.

### High-pass

Turns down everything below its **Frequency**. It starts at 80 Hz and 12 dB/oct, and its quick picks are 40, 60, 80, 100 and 120 Hz.

### Low shelf

Turns everything below its **Frequency** up or down by its **Gain**. It starts at 100 Hz and 0 dB, and its quick picks are 80, 100, 150 and 200 Hz.

### Band 1, 2 and 3

Each band turns the frequencies around its **Frequency** up or down by its **Gain**, and **Q** sets how narrow the affected range is. The bands start at 300 Hz, 1000 Hz and 4000 Hz, all at 0 dB and Q 1. All three have the same frequency quick picks: 100, 300, 1000, 2500, 4000 and 8000 Hz.

### High shelf

Turns everything above its **Frequency** up or down by its **Gain**. It starts at 8000 Hz and 0 dB, and its quick picks are 6000, 8000, 10000 and 12000 Hz.

### Low-pass

Turns down everything above its **Frequency**. It starts at 12000 Hz and 12 dB/oct, and its quick picks are 8000, 10000, 12000 and 16000 Hz.

## Frequency, Gain, Q and Slope

A part's controls are greyed out until you tick it. Which ones it has depends on its kind.

| Control | What it does | On | Range | Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Frequency** | Where the part acts: the corner of a pass or shelf, the centre of a band. It's a typed field with quick picks and no slider. | Every part | 20 to 20000 Hz | See [the seven parts](#the-seven-parts) |
| **Gain** | How far a shelf or band turns the sound up or down. Quick picks: −6, −3, 0, 3, 6. | Shelves and bands | −24 to +24 dB | 0 dB |
| **Q** | How narrow a band is. Higher is narrower. Quick picks: 0.5, 0.7, 1, 2, 4. | Bands | 0.1 to 10 | 1 |
| **Slope** | How steeply a pass falls away, in decibels per octave. 24 is the steeper. | High-pass and Low-pass | 12 dB/oct or 24 dB/oct | 12 dB/oct |

A number typed outside the range goes to the nearest end of it.

## The response curve

The first row of the section draws how much each frequency from 20 Hz to 20 kHz is turned up or down. The middle line is 0 dB. Upright lines mark 100 Hz, 1 kHz and 10 kHz, and level lines mark +12 and −12 dB. The curve covers ±24 dB, and anything past that sits at the edge.

The notches from [Hum removal](/audio/hum-removal/) are drawn on the same curve, so you see the two together.

The curve only shows the result. It has no handles to drag. It's greyed while Bypass is on, and it isn't drawn when the selected clips have different EQ or hum settings.

## Bypass

Tick **Bypass** to silence the EQ and keep its settings, so you can compare with and without. It's off by default, and it's greyed out until the clip has an EQ. Pressing a preset turns it off.

## Change the EQ over time

With an [action](/audio/animate-sound/) selected, the EQ's controls write the action's end. **Frequency**, **Gain** and **Q** move smoothly across the action. **Slope**, **Bypass** and turning a part on or off change at the action's end, with no glide. Unticking a part with an action selected turns it off at the end of the action.

**Frequency** has no slider, so when the value at the playhead differs from the one shown, the row says so in words, such as "now 450 Hz".

## Good to know

- The EQ runs after [Channels](/audio/channels/), and before [Hum removal](/audio/hum-removal/) and [Noise Reduction](/audio/noise-reduction/). [How audio works](/audio/how-audio-works/#the-fixed-signal-order) has the whole order.
- The EQ doesn't change the Loudness readout in the Volume section, but [Normalize](/audio/normalize/) in Loudness mode measures through it.
- [Enhance Voice](/audio/enhance-voice/) presses Rumble Cut for you. [Match Audio](/audio/match-audio/) with its **EQ** option on replaces the Low shelf, the three bands and the High shelf, keeps the High-pass and Low-pass, and turns Bypass off.
- At a cut between two clips, the EQ's settings switch with no glide.
- On a nest clip the EQ has no effect. Open the nest with <kbd>⌘↓</kbd> and set it on the clips inside.

## With an agent

`set_audio` sets the same parts through its `eq` argument. A part it leaves out keeps what it holds, `null` turns one part off, and `"eq": null` turns the whole EQ off. See [Tools: clip sound](/mcp/tools-set-audio/#eq).

Two things differ from the inspector. Turning a band on at its defaults starts it at 1000 Hz whichever band it is, where the inspector starts the three at 300, 1000 and 4000 Hz. And a value outside its range is an error that changes nothing, where the inspector moves it to the nearest end.

To switch one part off at the end of an action, an agent names it in the action's `off` list. See [Tools: animation](/mcp/tools-animation/#switching-off-at-an-end).
