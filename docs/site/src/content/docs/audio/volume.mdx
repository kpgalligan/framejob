The Volume section sets a clip's level and tells you how loud the clip is before and after that level. It's the first section on the [Sound tab](/audio/sound-tab/).

## Set the volume

Select the clip and open the **Volume** section. Drag the **Volume** slider, type in its field, or choose a value from the field's chevron: 0, 50, 100, 150, 200 or 400 %.

| Control | Default | Range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Volume** | 100 % | 0 to 3200 % (+30.1 dB) |

The slider stops at 400 %. To go higher, type in the field, up to 3200 %. Above 400 % the slider's knob stays at the right end. The high ceiling is there so that a quiet microphone recording can be brought up.

To change the level partway through a clip, [add a point](/audio/volume-line/#add-a-point) on the volume line. With an [action selected](/audio/animate-sound/), **Volume** writes the level the action ends on, not the clip's own.

## Mute a clip

Tick **Mute** to silence the clip without changing its volume. It's off by default.

A muted clip's waveform is drawn faded and its [volume line](/audio/volume-line/) lies along the bottom. To silence a whole track, use [Mute and solo tracks](/audio/mute-and-solo/).

With an action selected, **Mute** switches at the action's end, and the row shows "at end".

## Read the level

Three rows report the clip's level. You can't edit them.

### Gain

**Gain** is the **Volume** in decibels, such as "+3.0 dB", or "silent" at 0 %. At 100 % it reads "+0.0 dB".

### Loudness

**Loudness** is the clip's integrated loudness and true peak at volume 100 %, such as "−41.7 LUFS · −18.5 dBTP". It's measured to BS.1770-4 over the part of the file the clip plays.

On a stereo file, the reading includes the channel choice, Mix to mono and Pan. A mono clip counts as dual mono, as an export plays it, which reads about 3 dB louder than the file alone.

It leaves out **Volume**, fades, ducking, **Mute** and solo, the track's volume, and EQ, Hum and the rest of the clip's processing.

A clip at another speed, or a ramped one, reads within about 0.5 LU of what plays. A file with more than two channels is measured from a two-channel mix of it.

### At volume

**At volume** is the same reading with the clip's **Volume** applied, and through its [Limiter](/audio/limiter/) when that's on. With the limiter on, the true peak reads no higher than the ceiling. The row follows a **Volume** drag as you move.

The fast reading can't hear EQ, Hum, Voice Isolation, Noise Reduction, the Gate, the Leveler, the De-esser, the Compressor or a Voice Effect. On a clip using any of them, from its start or switched on by an action part-way through:

- If [Normalize](/audio/normalize/) in Loudness mode or [Enhance Voice](/audio/enhance-voice/) has measured the clip as it is now, the row reads that measurement.
- Otherwise the figure is the one before the processing and starts with "≈". Its tooltip says so.

**At volume** doesn't measure on its own, so "≈" stays until you press one of those two.

### What the readouts can show instead

| It reads | Because |
| --- | --- |
| "measuring…" | The clip's [sound analysis](/audio/how-audio-works/#sound-analysis-after-import) hasn't finished. |
| "—" | Several clips are selected, or a nest is, or the clip can't be measured. |
| "— LUFS" beside a peak | The clip plays for under 0.4 s, or it's quieter than −70 LUFS throughout. |
| "silent" (At volume) | **Volume** is 0 %. |

## Reset the section

**Reset** in the section's header puts **Volume** back to 100 % and turns **Mute** off. It leaves the Normalize row alone, and it leaves the [Limiter](/audio/limiter/) alone, including one Normalize turned on.

## Good to know

- [Normalize](/audio/normalize/) and [Enhance Voice](/audio/enhance-voice/) sit at the bottom of this section. Both set **Volume** for you, and both leave **Mute** alone.
- On a [nest clip](/audio/how-audio-works/#sound-on-a-nest), **Volume** and **Mute** scale everything inside it.
- A muted clip that ducks still lowers the others. See [Ducking](/audio/ducking/).
