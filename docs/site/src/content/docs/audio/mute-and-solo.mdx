## Mute a track

Click the speaker switch in the track's header. Every clip on the track goes silent, and the picture is unaffected. Click it again to bring the sound back.

**Mute Audio** in the track's right-click menu does the same for the track you clicked. **Track ▸ Mute Audio** acts on the [current track](/timeline/tracks/#the-current-track): the track of the first selected clip, or the front track when nothing is selected.

To silence one clip and not its whole track, use its own [**Mute**](/audio/volume/#mute-a-clip).

## Solo a track

Click the headphones switch in the track's header, or choose **Solo** in the track's right-click menu or the **Track** menu. The tracks beside it go silent: the other timeline tracks or, inside a nest, that nest's other tracks.

You can solo several tracks. All the soloed tracks play, and the rest are silenced.

**Solo** shows a checkmark in the **Track** menu while it's on. Neither **Solo** nor **Mute Audio** has a keyboard shortcut. Each click is one undo step, named "Mute Track" or "Solo Track".

## How mute and solo combine

- **Mute always wins.** A track that's both muted and soloed stays silent.
- **Solo doesn't look at what's on the track.** A soloed track with nothing on it still silences the rest, as a mixer's solo button does.
- **Pictures stay.** A silenced track still draws. To take a picture out, use [Hide Video](/timeline/hide-and-lock-tracks/#hide-video), which leaves the sound playing.

## See what a solo silences

Under a solo, the waveforms on silenced tracks draw faded, as they do on a muted track. Their speaker switches stay unlit, because a speaker shows that track's own setting and not what a solo is doing to it. So the waveform tells you what you'll hear, and the switch tells you what you've set.

## Mute and solo in nests

A solo only reaches the tracks beside it:

- A solo on a timeline track silences the other timeline tracks, and a nest playing on one of them goes silent with it.
- A solo on a track inside a [nest](/editing/nests/) affects only that nest's other tracks.

Muting the track a nest clip sits on silences everything inside the nest. A new nest's tracks copy the mute and solo of the tracks they came from, so a muted track's clips stay silent once they're nested. Un-nesting doesn't keep what's set on the nest's tracks.

## In an export

Mute and solo are part of the project, so the export hears what you hear. A silenced track isn't in the exported mix, and it writes no [stem](/export/stems/). If every track is silenced, Frame Job won't write stems.

:::caution
A solo left on silences the rest of the export. Check the header switches before you export.
:::

## Good to know

- The switches are plain toggles. <kbd>⌥</kbd>-clicking one does what a click does, and <kbd>⌥</kbd>-clicking the header away from its switches [selects the track's clips](/timeline/tracks/#the-track-header).
- A [scrub](/audio/scrub-audio/) plays the same mix, so mute and solo silence it too.
- The header's other switches are on [The track header](/timeline/tracks/#the-track-header).

## With an agent

`set_track` sets the same switches. Name the `track` and pass `muted` or `solo` as true or false. It's a direct setting, not a toggle, so asking for what's already set changes nothing. See [Tools: tracks, markers and canvas](/mcp/tools-tracks-and-markers/#set_track).
