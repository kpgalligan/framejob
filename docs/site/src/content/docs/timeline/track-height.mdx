Track height sets how tall the timeline's rows are. Go small to see more tracks at once, or large to get more room for filmstrips, waveforms and actions.

## Small, Medium, Large

Click **S**, **M** or **L** at the right end of the timeline bar, or choose a size from **View ▸ Timeline Track Height**. The menu checks the size you're on.

Medium is the default. Your choice is remembered between launches and applies to every project, because it's a view setting and not part of the project. It makes no undo step, and no agent tool sets it.

## Height follows contents

A track's height also depends on what it holds, so sound-only and text tracks draw shorter than picture tracks.

| Track holds | Small | Medium | Large |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Only text and shapes | 30 pt | 44 pt | 58 pt |
| Only clips with sound and no picture | 40 pt | 60 pt | 82 pt |
| Anything else, an empty track included | 54 pt | 84 pt | 116 pt |

The row changes as the track's contents do. A music track grows to picture height when you drop a video on it, for example. An adjustment clip, a graphic or a nest counts as picture.

A clip's own switches count. A detached sound, with its picture off, is a clip with sound and no picture.

## What each size shows

Every clip keeps its 16 pt name strip, and the rest of the row goes to its filmstrip, its waveform and its actions. So at Small a clip shows little more than its name: 8 pt is left under the strip on a text row, 18 pt on a sound row and 32 pt on a picture row.

The action lane under the name strip changes with the size:

| Size | Lane height | Each action band shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small | 5 pt | A plain bar. |
| Medium | 12 pt | Its number in time order, and a zoom glyph for a zoom action. |
| Large | 18 pt | The same, plus the properties it changes. |

See [Reading a clip on the timeline](/timeline/reading-a-clip/) for what each part of a clip means.

## Good to know

- A size change applies to the window you made it in. Other windows that are already open keep their size, and the next one you open uses your latest choice.
- While you drag a clip, lanes keep their heights, so the track you're aiming at doesn't move under the pointer. They settle when you drop.
