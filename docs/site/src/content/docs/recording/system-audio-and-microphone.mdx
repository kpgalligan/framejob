A recording can carry two sounds: what your Mac plays, and your voice. Frame Job keeps them apart, so you can set the level of each on its own after the recording lands.

## Record what your Mac plays

**System Audio** records the sound from every app but Frame Job, as the movie's own stereo 48 kHz sound track. It's on until you turn it off, and Frame Job remembers your choice.

With it off, the movie has no sound track at all. The switch applies to the screen, so **Camera** and **Microphone Only** in **Source** grey it out, keeping its value.

## Play the timeline while you record

Frame Job's own sound is never recorded, whether or not **Include Frame Job windows** is on. You can play your timeline during a recording and its sound won't be in the file.

## Record your voice

Choose a microphone in **Microphone**. **Off** comes first and is the default, then every connected microphone by name. Frame Job remembers your choice, and falls back to **Off** if that microphone isn't connected.

It works with every source. With a picture, the voice goes into a second file beside the movie, named after it with ` (Microphone)` added, and never into the movie. With a microphone chosen, pressing **Record** also checks the [Microphone permission](/recording/permissions/).

To clean your voice up as it lands, tick [**Enhance the microphone when it lands**](/recording/when-it-lands/). To record your voice with no picture, see [Record a voice-over](/recording/voice-overs/).

## Check your level

A meter sits beside **Microphone**. It starts moving at the countdown, not when you choose the microphone, so the 3, 2, 1 is your chance to check your level before the recording starts.

It stays live through the recording, and goes silent while paused. For a display or a window the panel leaves the screen once recording begins, so you only see the meter during the countdown. For a camera on its own or a voice-over, the panel stays up and so does the meter.

## Your first words are kept

The microphone starts during the countdown, not when the recording does, so your first words are in the file. A camera's capture starts early the same way. Both are let go if you cancel the countdown, close the panel, or the start fails.

## How the microphone lands

Each file lands on one new track at the back, named **Microphone**, lined up with its picture, so your voice and the computer's sound have separate volumes.

Frame Job measures how far apart the two files started, for each stretch of a [paused recording](/recording/pause-and-stop/#paused-recordings-are-stretches) separately, and places the clip to match. The alignment is exact to half a frame.

If your voice started before its clip could, at the very start of the timeline or right after the previous microphone clip, the clip's head is trimmed rather than the clip being placed late.

If no voice can be placed, no empty track is added. [Where a recording lands](/recording/where-it-lands/) covers the undo steps.

## If the microphone stops

A microphone that fails doesn't stop a screen or camera recording. The picture keeps going, the meter goes silent, and the panel's note reads "The microphone stopped: … The picture is still recording." That stretch's microphone file isn't added to the project. It stays on disk, and the note names it when the recording lands.

A [voice-over](/recording/voice-overs/#good-to-know) is different. With nothing else recording, it ends there.

## Plug devices in and take them out

The **Camera**, **Format** and **Microphone** lists follow the devices you plug in or take away.

- Changes apply only while the panel is idle, so nothing moves during a countdown, a recording or a landing. A change that waited applies when the panel goes idle.
- A change made while the panel was closed is picked up when it next shows.
- A microphone you take away falls back to **Off**. A camera falls back to the default camera, or to **None connected** when there's no camera left.
- When a device comes back it's chosen again, and a camera comes back with its stored format.
- Plugging in or removing a device doesn't change what the panel remembers. The next control you change saves whatever the lists show at that moment, so a device that's away at that point is forgotten. The one exception is **None connected**, which leaves the remembered camera alone.
- A list is rebuilt only when its own devices changed, so a menu you have open isn't disturbed by an unrelated device.

## With an agent

An agent turns each sound on or off rather than choosing a device. Its microphone is your Mac's default microphone, off unless it asks. It can't ask for the Microphone permission either: that takes one press of **Record** in the panel. A microphone that fails doesn't fail an agent's recording, and the result says why. See [Record with record_screen](/mcp/record-screen/#sound).
