The **Appearance** section of the [Document tab](/projects/document-tab/) sets three things for the whole project: the colour behind your clips, how times are written, and how big a new clip starts.

<AtAGlance where="Document tab ▸ Appearance" tools={['set_canvas', 'add_clip']} />

| Control | What it does | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Background** | The colour behind every layer. | Black, opaque |
| **Timecode** | How the project shows a time. | Frames |
| **Fit new clips to canvas** | Scales down a new clip that's bigger than the canvas. | On |

A change to **Timecode** or **Fit new clips to canvas** is one undo step.

## Set the background colour

Click the **Background** swatch and pick a colour in the Colors panel. The colour sits behind every layer, so it shows wherever no clip covers the canvas.

To put a colour on the timeline as a clip instead, see [Backgrounds](/canvas/backgrounds/).

## Make the background transparent

Lower the opacity in the Colors panel. The swatch shows a checkerboard behind the colour, and the **Background** row of the [Document summary](/projects/document-tab/#the-document-summary) reads `Transparent`, or the colour with its opacity.

Most export formats can't carry transparency, so while the background isn't fully opaque, the section adds a note about the formats that can.

| The note says | When |
| --- | --- |
| "Transparent — kept by 4 export formats." | Three or more formats keep it, so the note counts them. They're HEVC with Alpha (MOV), VP9 with Alpha (WebM), ProRes 4444 (MOV) and Animated PNG. |
| "Transparent — needs an alpha format." | Frame Job can't name the formats yet, which can happen on a project with nothing on the timeline. |

See [Transparent exports](/export/transparency/).

## Choose a timecode format

**Timecode** sets how times are written everywhere the project shows one, including the toolbar, the ruler, the inspector, markers and the transcript.

| Choice | Looks like |
| --- | --- |
| **Frames** | `HH:MM:SS:FF` |
| **Milliseconds** | `HH:MM:SS.mmm` |

**Frames** doesn't drop frames, so at 29.97 fps the count runs slightly behind the clock over a long timeline.

The count starts at zero, and there's no start-timecode setting. Frame Job doesn't read a file's source timecode, and an export has no timecode track. To put the timecode in a file, [burn it in](/export/burn-in-timecode/).

## Fit new clips to canvas

This decides how big a new clip starts. It's on by default.

| Setting | A clip bigger than the canvas | Any other clip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| On | Starts at Fit scale: scaled down until all of it shows. | Starts at 100 %, its native size. |
| Off | Starts at 100 %, so it runs past the edges of the canvas. | Starts at 100 %. |

A clip counts as bigger when either its width or its height is larger than the canvas. Fit never scales a clip up, and changing the setting leaves clips already on the timeline as they are. To fit or fill one clip, see [Position, scale, rotation and opacity](/canvas/transform/).

## Reset the section

**Reset** on the **Appearance** header puts all three back to their defaults: opaque black, **Frames** and on. It's offered only while one of them differs, and it's one undo step.

## With an agent

[`set_canvas`](/mcp/tools-tracks-and-markers/#set_canvas) sets all three, and anything it doesn't name keeps its value.

| Argument | What it takes |
| --- | --- |
| `background` | Four numbers from 0 to 1, `[r, g, b, a]`. Three numbers, or one outside the range, fails and nothing changes. |
| `timecode_frames` | `true` for **Frames**, `false` for **Milliseconds**. |
| `fit_new_clips` | `true` or `false`, as the checkbox. |

[`add_clip`](/mcp/tools-adding-clips/#add_clip) follows **Fit new clips to canvas** as the app does. See [Tools: tracks, markers and canvas](/mcp/tools-tracks-and-markers/).
