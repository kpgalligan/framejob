The Light section corrects how bright a clip is and how warm it looks, then shapes its tones. It's usually where a grade starts.

## The Light section

<Steps>

1. Select the clip and open the [**Color**](/color/color-tab/) sub-tab.

2. Open the **Light** section by clicking its header, if it isn't open already.

3. Drag a slider, or type in the number field beside it.

</Steps>

All nine controls start at 0, and each shows two decimals. The rows run in the order they act on the picture, so working down the list is a natural way to correct a clip.

| Control | Range | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Exposure** | −3 to +3 EV | Brightens or darkens in stops |
| **Temperature** | −1 to +1 | Warmer or cooler |
| **Tint** | −1 to +1 | Toward magenta or green |
| **Highlights** | −1 to +1 | Lifts or lowers the brighter half of the tones |
| **Shadows** | −1 to +1 | Lifts or lowers the darker half of the tones |
| **Whites** | −1 to +1 | Stretches the tones toward white, with black held |
| **Blacks** | −1 to +1 | Raises or deepens the dark end, with white held |
| **Vibrance** | −1 to +1 | Changes saturation mostly in colours that have little of it |
| **Fade** | 0 to 1 | Lifts black and lowers white, for a washed-out film look |

**Reset** in the header sets all nine back to 0 as one undo step, **Undo Light**, and leaves the [Color section](/color/color-section/) alone. The header reads "neutral" or "adjusted".

With an [action](/color/animate-a-grade/) selected, every control here sets the value the action animates to, rather than the clip's own value.

## Set the exposure

**Exposure** brightens or darkens the picture in stops, from −3 to +3 EV. +1 doubles the light and −1 halves it.

It works on linear light, meaning the amount of light in the scene rather than the encoded picture values, the way a camera's exposure does. So black stays black. To lift every level by the same amount, black included, use [Brightness](/color/color-section/#brightness).

To judge exposure by measurement rather than by eye, watch the [waveform](/color/waveform-and-parade/) as you drag.

## Set the white balance

**Temperature** and **Tint** work on linear light, like Exposure. Both run from −1 to +1.

| Control | Positive | Negative | At the limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Temperature** | Warmer: more red, less blue | Cooler: more blue, less red | At +1 red rises and blue falls by 0.3 of a stop. −1 is the reverse. |
| **Tint** | Toward magenta | Toward green | At +1 green falls by 0.3 of a stop, which reads as magenta. −1 raises it. |

There's no white-balance eyedropper, and no automatic colour balance or shot matching. To balance by measurement, read something neutral on the [Parade](/color/waveform-and-parade/#read-the-rgb-parade) and move these two until the channels line up.

## Lift or lower the highlights and shadows

**Highlights** moves the brighter half of the tones and **Shadows** the darker half. Both run from −1 to +1, and neither touches the other's half.

At ±1 each moves its tones by up to a quarter of the full range. The move is strongest at the extreme, white for Highlights and black for Shadows, and fades to nothing at the middle of the tonal range.

## Set the white and black points

**Whites** stretches the tones toward white and holds black where it is. **Blacks** does the same at the other end: positive raises the dark end, negative deepens it, and white is held. Both run from −1 to +1.

Each is a stretch of up to 15 %.

## Add colour with Vibrance

**Vibrance** changes saturation mostly in colours that have little of it, from −1 to +1. Colours that are already strong are left mostly alone, which is the difference from [Saturation](/color/color-section/#saturation), which moves every colour alike.

At +1 a nearly grey colour gets about twice its saturation, and at −1 it goes grey. A fully saturated colour doesn't change at either end.

## Fade the picture

**Fade** lifts black and lowers white for a washed-out film look. It runs from 0 to 1 and has no negative side. At 1, black rises to 10 % and white falls to 95 %.

## With an agent

One `set_video` call can set any of the nine Light controls and the four [Color](/color/color-section/) controls together, in its `color` object, and it leaves the ones it doesn't name alone. Each key is its control's name in lower case, such as `exposure` or `fade`, and 0 means no change. A positive `temperature` is warmer and a positive `tint` is more magenta. `set_action_end` takes the same keys for an action.

A `set_video` call with a value outside a Light control's range fails, rather than being pulled back to the limit as a typed number is. `set_action_end` does take such a value, and the action stops at the limit when it's drawn. See [Tools: picture and grade](/mcp/tools-set-video/#color).

## Good to know

- A number you type outside a control's range is pulled back to the nearest limit.
- Light acts after the Color section's four controls, even though the tab lists it first. See [The fixed grade order](/color/color-tab/#the-fixed-grade-order).
- On an [adjustment clip](/color/adjustment-clips/), Light grades everything behind the clip.
- In an [HDR project](/hdr/dynamic-range/), Exposure works on the scene's light as HLG encodes it, so +1 still doubles the light.
