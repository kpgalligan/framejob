## The Curves section

Select a video, image or still clip, or an [adjustment clip](/color/adjustment-clips/), open the [Color tab](/color/color-tab/) and open **Curves**. The section starts closed, and its header reads "neutral" until a curve changes, then "adjusted".

The section has three parts, from top to bottom:

- The channel picker, which chooses the curve you're editing.
- The plot, a square with a 4 × 4 grid. It grows with the inspector up to 300 pt wide.
- The selected point's **In** and **Out** fields, and **Delete**.

Across the plot is the level going in, from 0 at the left to 1 at the right. Up the plot is the level coming out. A thin diagonal marks a straight curve, which changes nothing.

## The channel picker

Six cells above the plot choose the channel you edit. Click a different cell, and any selected point is deselected.

| Cell | What it changes |
| --- | --- |
| **Master** | All three colours together. Use it for contrast and for the black and white points. |
| **Red** | The red channel only. |
| **Green** | The green channel only. |
| **Blue** | The blue channel only. |
| **Hue** | Hue vs Hue, which turns a chosen hue toward another. See [Hue vs Hue and Hue vs Sat curves](/color/hue-curves/). |
| **Sat** | Hue vs Sat, which saturates or mutes a chosen hue. See [Hue vs Hue and Hue vs Sat curves](/color/hue-curves/). |

**Master**, **Red**, **Green** and **Blue** are the tone curves, and **Hue** and **Sat** are the hue curves.

There's no separate luma curve. **Master** runs first, then each colour goes through its own channel, so a **Red** curve works on what **Master** has already done.

The chosen cell is framed and labelled in its channel's colour. A new window starts on **Master**, and the plot stays on your channel while the window is open. The choice isn't saved with the project.

### See which channels are changed

A dot under a cell means that channel has been changed.

Changed channels also show as faint lines in their own colour behind the curve you're editing. **Master**, **Red**, **Green** and **Blue** show behind each other. **Hue** and **Sat** each have their own scale, so neither shows behind the other.

## Shape the picture

| To | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Add contrast | On **Master**, make a gentle S: pull a point low on the curve down, and a point high on the curve up. |
| Set the black point | On **Master**, drag the first point right along the bottom. Everything darker than its **In** comes out black. |
| Set the white point | On **Master**, drag the last point left along the top. Everything brighter than its **In** comes out white. |
| Lift the blacks | On **Master**, drag the first point up the left edge. |
| Warm the shadows | On **Red**, drag the first point up the left edge. |
| Take out a colour cast | On the channel that's too strong, add a point in the middle of the curve and pull it down. |

## Edit points

A curve holds from 2 to 16 points. It runs smoothly through every point and never overshoots, so between two points it stays between their values. Past the first and last points, a tone curve stays flat.

Each drag, nudge, deletion or typed value is one undo step, and **Edit ▸ Undo** reads **Undo Curves**.

### Add a point

Click the plot away from any point. Frame Job adds a point on the curve, directly above or below your click, and selects it. Keep the button down and the same press drags it.

A double-click on the curve itself adds one point. A double-click away from the curve adds two, side by side, because the second click misses the first point.

A 17th point beeps, and so does a click with no room left between two points.

On a straight channel, the new point isn't saved until you move it, nudge it or type a value, so a stray click changes nothing. It stays on the plot while it's selected, and it goes when you deselect it or change channel. You can click several in a row to pin a straight curve before you bend it, and they're all saved with the first one you move.

On a channel that already has a shape, the click saves the new point straight away. The point lies on the curve, so the picture changes very little, if at all.

### Drag a point

Press a point to grab it where it stands, with no jump, and drag. It stays inside the plot and can't pass its neighbours. The closest it gets to a neighbour across the plot is 0.001 on a tone curve and 0.1° on a hue curve.

You can drag the end points too.

### Fine drag and axis lock

Hold <kbd>⌥</kbd> while you drag to move the point at a tenth of the speed.

Hold <kbd>⇧</kbd> while you drag to lock the point to one axis. It stays put until the pointer has travelled 4 pt from the press, and then whichever direction you moved further wins for the rest of the drag. Let go of <kbd>⇧</kbd> and the point follows the pointer again.

<kbd>⌥</kbd> and <kbd>⇧</kbd> combine.

### Nudge a point

With a point selected, the arrow keys nudge it. Hold <kbd>⇧</kbd> to go ten times as far. Each press is one undo step.

| Channel | Arrow key | With <kbd>⇧</kbd> |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Master**, **Red**, **Green**, **Blue** | 0.01 | 0.1 |
| **Hue** | 1° | 10° |
| **Sat** | 1° across, 0.01 up and down | 10° across, 0.1 up and down |

### In and Out fields

The **In** and **Out** fields below the plot let you type the selected point's position. With no point selected, they're blank and disabled.

| Channel | **In** | **Out** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Master**, **Red**, **Green**, **Blue** | 0 to 1, three decimals | 0 to 1, three decimals |
| **Hue** | 0° to 359.9°, one decimal | −180° to +180°, one decimal |
| **Sat** | 0° to 359.9°, one decimal | 0× to 2×, two decimals |

A number outside the range goes to the nearest end of it, and an **In** past a neighbouring point stops short of that point.

### Delete a point

Select the point and press <kbd>⌫</kbd> or <kbd>⌦</kbd>, choose **Delete** below the plot, or double-click the point.

A curve can't go below two points. The keys and the double-click beep, and **Delete** is greyed out.

### Deselect a point

Press <kbd>Esc</kbd>, or click another channel. This also hands the keyboard back to the editor.

### The plot and the keyboard

While a point is selected the plot has the keyboard, so:

- The arrow keys nudge the point rather than move the playhead.
- <kbd>⌫</kbd> deletes the point, not the selected clip.

Keys the plot doesn't use still reach the editor, so <kbd>Space</kbd> still plays.

## Hover readout

While the pointer is over the plot, and during a drag, a small label shows the pointer's position and the curve's value there.

| Channel | The label reads |
| --- | --- |
| **Master**, **Red**, **Green**, **Blue** | `In 0.42 → Out 0.47` |
| **Hue** | `Hue 34° → +12°` |
| **Sat** | `Hue 120° → ×0.80` |

There's no label outside the plot, and that includes the spectrum strip under a hue channel.

## Reset curves

To straighten one channel, right-click its cell in the picker and choose **Reset Master**, **Reset Red**, **Reset Green**, **Reset Blue**, **Reset Hue** or **Reset Sat**. The item is greyed out while that channel is already straight.

To straighten all six channels, choose **Reset** in the section's header. It shows while a curve is changed, and it's one undo step. You can also right-click the plot and choose **Reset to default**.

## Curves inside an action

With an [action](/motion/actions/) selected, a curve edit goes to the action's end, so the curve animates. Any two curves animate into each other, whatever their number of points, and so does a curve and a straight line.

The plot shows it in its own way: the action's start curve is dashed, and the edited curve and its points are in the action colour.

To animate a curve back to neutral, right-click a cell and choose its **Reset** item, or right-click the plot and choose **Set end value to default**. Each writes a straight curve to the action's end, for the channels that aren't already straight. The section's **Reset** isn't there while the action is selected. **Clear** takes its place once the action animates curves, and removes them from the action.

See [Animate a grade](/color/animate-a-grade/).

## Good to know

With several clips selected, the plot shows the first clip's curves, and the section shows **mixed** when they differ. An edit gives every selected clip the curve you're editing, for that channel, and leaves their other channels alone.

Selecting another clip deselects the point.

## With an agent

An agent writes a whole channel at once as a list of 2 to 16 points, and that list replaces the channel. Sending `null` for a channel removes its curve, and a list that changes nothing is stored as no curve.

A drag stops at a limit, but an agent's value outside the limits gets an error and the curve stays as it was. An action's end is the exception: the value is kept, and it's moved to the nearest limit as the action plays. See [Tools: picture and grade](/mcp/tools-set-video/#curves).
