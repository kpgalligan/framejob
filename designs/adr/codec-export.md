## Context

Before this road map Frame Job exported H.264, HEVC (8-bit, 10-bit, with alpha), ProRes and still sequences, all through AVFoundation and VideoToolbox. The owner's brief (2026-10-27) asked for AV1 and VP9 with the encoders bundled, and fixed four rows (AV1 in MP4 with AAC, AV1 in WebM with Opus, VP9 in WebM with Opus, VP9 with alpha in WebM; VP9 in MP4 not wanted), SVT-AV1 at three speeds (Faster 8, Balanced 6, Smaller file 4), libvpx at one, and a Rust WebM writer in the core rather than libwebm. The rest were the orchestrator's defaults, each the owner's to change: AudioToolbox's Opus, AV1 HDR first and VP9 Profile 2 later, pinned tarballs with no vendored source, the bitrate semantics kept, the M1/M2 decode gap documented, the encoders platform-side. Standing owner rules applied throughout: only higher-quality exports matter, memory limits relative to physical RAM, no feature size budget, person-only checks in `docs/manual-checks.md`. (orig "Goal", "The owner's choices", "The orchestrator's defaults", "How it fits the tree (at `75fd806`)")

## Decisions

- **SVT-AV1, not libaom (CX-2, the spike).** At matched quality SVT-AV1 preset 4 equalled libaom good-5/6 (Δ VMAF over HEVC +3.36 against +3.67/+3.57 on `real8`, +5.11 against +4.96/+4.88 on `gh5`) at about 41 fps against 13.6–16, about 3× faster; at matched speed SVT 8 gave +2.56 against libaom rt-8's +1.37. libaom's best (good-3/4) is about 0.8 VMAF above SVT 4 at about 11× the time. libaom's favour: a higher worst frame (92 against 88) and half the memory. Presets 10 and 12 came within 0.7 VMAF of HEVC: no row. (orig S §2)
- **VP9 is for the web and alpha, not efficiency (CX-3).** libvpx good-4 needs 16–26 % less bitrate than HEVC for its quality, but 1.4–1.7× SVT 6's; good-2 bought +0.14–0.43 VMAF for about 35 % more time, rt-8 only HEVC's quality. (orig S §2)
- **The encoders are static, platform-side, built by `make codecs` (CX-4, CX-5).** No nasm, yasm, meson or ninja is needed on arm64 (Neon C intrinsics); cmake from Homebrew and the system perl are. The cold build is about 30 s; the stamp makes a warm one a no-op. Linked and stripped, SVT-AV1 adds 3.33 MB and libvpx 1.59 MB. `libSvtAv1Enc.a` carries `vector.c` (MIT), whose notice ships only through #376's acknowledgements (PUB-10). (orig S §1, C0)
- **The WebM writer is the core's (CX-6, CX-15).** Container bytes are decisions (timestamps, clusters, cues, CodecDelay), not sample work, as #216-R drew the line; the spike's libwebm files read back through the core's reader, which is the round-trip the core's tests now make against the writer. It is a second `ExportPipeline`, the one exception to §7.8's one writer, sharing the reader outputs, the pump, the gain, #337's end, the chapter partition, the size and the rates. (orig S §3, C3)
- **AV1 in MP4 goes through a passthrough `AVAssetWriterInput` (CX-12).** `canAdd` answered true for `av01`, and every frame decoded md5-identical. The core builds `av1C` from a full parse of the sequence header OBU, strips temporal delimiters and patches the `ftyp` to carry `av01`, which AV1-ISOBMFF requires and `AVAssetWriter` does not write (`rc_av1_*`). The writer reads sync samples from the bitstream itself: both `all-sync` and `no-sync` survived, so the attachment code went and A5's key-flag match is the guard. (orig S §3, C1, C1 fix pass)
- **AV1's rate is a soft cap, quality first (CX-13; the owner chose option 2, 2026-10-28).** Single-pass VBR (CX-9) undershot a real clip to 0.667–0.670 of its target. Capped CRF replaced it, calibrated so each tier matches or beats HEVC's VMAF at HEVC's rate on the spike's three clips and on the owner's 55 s 1080p clip through the app itself, because SvtAv1EncApp's command-line scores did not transfer (1.1 VMAF lower for SVT-AV1 at one CRF and 1.6 for HEVC on that clip). A strict cap (12 % overshoot) left Faster under HEVC at Format default (a plateau at 95.055–95.348 against 95.469), because SVT-AV1 budgets each window's keyframes and base layers under the cap; SVT's default 50 % overshoot met every comparator. (orig C1 fix pass)
- **`av1` is always 10-bit, an HDR row (CX-14).** AV1's Main profile decodes 8 and 10 bits alike and only higher-quality exports matter, so there is no `av1_10bit`. The CICP is written in both the sequence header and `colr`, HDR10 (HD-5's fixed values) in both SVT-AV1's metadata OBUs and `mdcv`/`clli`; ffprobe's MP4 colour comes from `colr`, so H2's `trace_headers` is what catches a mislabelled bitstream. The owner-clip half of the CRF table was re-calibrated against `hevc_10bit`, which scores above 8-bit `hevc` (96.075 against 95.469 at Format default); the spike half stands at 8 bits. (orig C2)
- **G-17 closed on the WebM path by one reader per output (CX-15 (2)).** C3 met one hang, a video pump blocked in `copyNextSampleBuffer` with the audio pump gone (#379). Cancelling a shared reader from the other thread is what G-17 measured crashing 12 of 30 cancels, so each pump now reads and cancels its own `AVAssetReader`; R1 measured the two-reader read equal frame for frame and sample for sample. #378 then did the same for `ExportWriter`. Every WebM stop is logged (`export.webm`), the Opus FIFO is fed by bytes, and the sound is placed at its stamps (`ExportAudioEnd.align`), not by count. (orig C3, C3 fix pass)
- **VP9's depth is the picture's, and the WebM Colour is its only transfer label (CX-16).** `sdr` is Profile 0 8-bit I420 BT.709; `hlg`/`pq` are Profile 2 10-bit I010 BT.2020. libvpx's bitstream carries a colour space but no transfer, so HLG and PQ live only in the container. (orig S §7, C4)
- **An SDR WebM copy is labelled from its source (CX-16 (10), RULING-1).** V10 re-imported Frame Job's own 640 × 360 SDR `vp9_webm` with cyan coming back (23, 255, 255): BT.709 decoded as BT.601, because the rewrap's copy carried no colour and AVFoundation chose the matrix by size. The copy now carries the Colour's codes, else a VP9 keyframe's `color_space` for the matrix (`stream_matrix`); V10 then read 1.90 / 0.55 / 0.96 of 255. (orig C4 "RULING-1")
- **VP9 alpha is a second encoder (CX-17).** libvpx has no alpha input; the alpha plane is a second 8-bit picture (Y = round(255·a), full range, chroma 128) at constant quality, keys forced on the same frames, packets paired by pts, written as BlockAdditional 1 with `AlphaMode` 1 and a ReferenceBlock on non-keys. AV1 alpha does not exist in practice (no API input, no Matroska mapping). The frames are straightened first (G-35). The alpha level is banded by size so every band holds 48 dB at its worst frame (C5 fix pass, RULING-1). (orig S §4, C5, C5 fix pass)
- **The memory limit is a ledger, not a pool (CX-18).** The queue stays one job at a time; a bundled-encoder job is held while the app's running encoder exports (the queue's and every Export… sheet's) and it would pass a quarter of physical memory, each costed by its measured peak per key, scaled by pixels. A sheet is counted, never held; a held job is passed over and says why. On a 32 GiB Mac that admits one AV1 job at 4K at any speed; on 16 GiB one AV1 4K job always runs alone. (orig C6a)
- **The estimate is fitted on real footage (CX-18 amended by C6b).** C6a's single-run constants, on AV.mov scaled up, were 50–79 % short of real footage. C6b took idle three-run medians on the GH5 4K clip, fitting a fixed start from a 250-frame and a 50-frame batch; `av1_webm` copies `av1`'s times (within 10 %). (orig C6a, C6b)

## Known limits

- **AV1 cannot be decoded on an M1 or M2** (CX-10): the export works, but Frame Job cannot preview or re-import its own AV1 file there, and the proofs read AV1 through ffmpeg; playback is SE-17 and SE-18 on an M3 or later.
- **A soft cap, not a ceiling** (CX-13, CX-16 (7)): a file usually stays under its rate but can pass it, measured up to 1.254 × on AV1 and 1.143 × on VP9; the guide says about 1.25 × and 1.15 ×. The calibration is four clips at 1080p, one of them binding, and other sizes are placed by bits per pixel alone.
- **Faster at Maximum sits 0.059 VMAF under `hevc_10bit`** (CX-14 (6), the owner's "Best CRF, accept 0.06"): below CRF 12 the cap binds and a lower CRF gains nothing.
- **Thin margins against a moving comparator**: VideoToolbox's `hevc` and `hevc_10bit` read a few hundredths differently run to run (95.338 and 95.469; one 10-bit encode 0.25 lower under load), and VP9's 8-bit Medium (+0.016) and Format default (+0.029) cells are kept by that much.
- **The spike half of AV1's calibration stands at 8 bits** (CX-14 (6)); only the owner-clip half was re-run against `hevc_10bit`.
- **A `vp9_alpha_webm` re-imports opaque** (CX-17 (7)): the rewrap copies the Block and not the BlockAdditional, and Apple's VP9 decoder has no alpha (#381). ffmpeg's native `vp9` decoder ignores the alpha too; only libvpx's reads it. Safari's playback is SE-21's question.
- **No HDR or 10-bit alpha, no AV1 alpha, no VP9 in MP4** (CX-1, CX-17 (8)).
- **One VP9 speed** (CX-3, CX-16 (2)): the Speed popup is disabled on the VP9 rows.
- **The HDR rendering is a person's** (HDR-8): ffmpeg's Matroska demuxer exports no content-light-level side data when MaxFALL is 0, which HD-5's fixed values make it, though the walk reads MaxCLL 1000 / MaxFALL 0 in the Colour.
- **Opus undershoots its rate**: AudioToolbox at 192 kb/s wrote about 119 kb/s on A1's sound (CX-7).
- **The cost tables are one machine and one picture** (CX-18): a heavier timeline or harder footage can exceed a peak or take longer, and a sheet export can take the app past the budget (CX-18 (6)). VP9's estimate runs 12–14 % high at 1440p, its per-frame time growing slower than the pixels between the two measured sizes; within the tolerance and not re-tuned.
- **GIF and APNG are outside the ledger**: a 4K GIF peaked 11.48 GiB and a 4K APNG 7.85 GiB above baseline (C6b's A), neither booked because neither is a writer row (#383).
