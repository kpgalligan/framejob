# 4.3b Inspector — the Clip tab's sections: video, audio, text and shape

The tab strip, the Clip tab's sub-tabs, pinned block, row marks and Reset/Clear are §4.3a; the Media, Document and Assistant tabs are §4.3c.

The section groups below are named as they were when each was a tab; they
are now sections of the Clip tab, offered when the selection has that aspect
and grouped into the sub-tabs of §4.3a.

**Video sections** — any clip with a video property group, which is every
clip that draws. A text or shape clip therefore gets Transform and the
whole effects group (Shadow, Filters, Reflection, Spotlight, Obscure) too:
those measure the layer against its own source pixels, and a title has
those as much as a movie does. What it does NOT get is the sections tied to
a source that has pixels of its own — Crop, Corner radius, Colour and
Chroma key — or to a media source, which is Speed.

- *Transform*: Scale % (1–1000), Position X / Y in canvas pixels (origin
  at canvas center, X right, Y down), Rotation °, Opacity %, and — format
  version 6 — **Blend**, a popup over the eight modes of §5.2's
  `video.blend`, beside Opacity because the two are the same question
  about how this layer meets what is under it. Buttons: Fit to canvas,
  Fill canvas. The one trap the feature has is answered in place rather
  than in a manual: every mode but `normal` is a function of TWO colours,
  so a layer with nothing behind it over the default black canvas either
  reads black (`multiply`, `darken`, `overlay` all collapse against 0) or
  changes nothing at all (`screen`, `add`, `lighten`, `difference` are the
  identity against 0). The section's note is COMPUTED — it appears only
  when there really is no visible layer behind the clip and the canvas
  background really is black, and it names which of the two failures is on
  screen — and the popup's tooltip carries the whole of it and the answer,
  which is Clip ▸ Add Background. `blend` is an ORDINARY animatable key,
  deliberately not one the list rule's structural exception pulls out: a
  discrete value inside an action holds and switches at the end, which is
  what §5.4 says a discrete value does.
- *Crop*: left / top / right / bottom in source pixels; a "Crop on
  canvas" button enters crop mode.
- *Corner radius* px, applied to the cropped rectangle before rotation.
- *Shadow*: on/off, color, opacity, blur, angle, distance.
- *Adjustment* (the Color sub-tab's first section for an all-adjustment
  selection, `video.adjustment`, §11 GR-6): one row, Strength — the clip's
  `opacity` as 0–100 %, a slider and a field with the quick values 0, 25, 50,
  75 and 100; summary the strength ("75%"); Reset sets full strength, undo
  "Adjustment". The point filters (sepia, noir, vignette, invert) have no row
  and are reached through MCP and Paste Attributes.
- *Qualifier* (the Color sub-tab's second section for an all-adjustment
  selection, `video.qualifier`, §11 GR-13), closed by default: per range — Hue,
  Saturation, Luma, in that order — two rows: the range's checkbox in the
  76 pt label column (ticking writes `{}`, the core's default: hue {30, 50,
  25}, the levels {0, 1, 0.1}; unticking `null`; one undo step named after the
  range) then its **strip** (`RangeStripControl`): 14 pt tall, square-framed,
  the hue spectrum at full saturation, a white → red ramp or black → white,
  dimmed 60 % outside the range and its softness and 30 % in the soft bands;
  the range's edges are the slider's 7 × 11 block knobs and the softness
  edges 5 × 5 squares; a knob drags the width (hue, symmetric about the
  centre) or low/high, a square the softness (symmetric), the band's body
  moves the range (hue wraps); ⌥ held at a tenth; a double-click resets the
  range to its default; each gesture the live trio, one undo step named after
  the range; keyed, the band is outlined and the knobs drawn in action ink.
  Then three captioned 41 pt fields — Centre, Width, Soft in whole degrees, or
  Low, High, Soft to two decimals — dimmed while the range is off. Then
  Invert, then **Blur** (§11 GR-35: the house slider and field, 0–100 %, quick
  values 0/25/50/75/100, one undo step "Qualifier Blur"; 100 % is a Gaussian
  of σ 24 canvas pixels; no ⌥-fine drag or double-click, only the strips have
  them), then a "Pick from Canvas" button (a canvas mode: one click on the
  preview samples the picture behind the clip and centres the ranges on it,
  undo "Qualifier"; Esc or a selection change cancels) beside a "Show
  Selection" checkbox (View ▸ Show Selection, §9). Every write sends that
  range whole to every selected clip; a mixed selection shows the first
  clip's values with the mixed badge. Summary "off" or the ranges on ("hue ·
  sat · luma", plus " · inverted", plus " · blur 40%" when the blur is above
  0); Reset turns every range and Invert off and the blur to 0, undo
  "Qualifier". A pick leaves the blur alone.
- *Color* (the Color sub-tab's second section, §11 GR-2): brightness,
  contrast, saturation, hue (an HSV-hue turn in degrees, + red → yellow →
  green, luma kept, §11 GR-33); each row writes only its own key; the
  summary and Reset are about these four alone.
- *Light* (the Color sub-tab's first section, `video.light`, §11 GR-2):
  Exposure (−3…+3, "EV"), Temperature, Tint, Highlights, Shadows, Whites,
  Blacks, Vibrance (each −1…+1) and Fade (0…1), a slider and a 2-decimal field
  each, default 0; each row writes only its own key, so a multi-selection
  keeps every clip's other values; summary "neutral"/"adjusted"; Reset zeroes
  the nine and nothing of Color's; undo names are the row labels and Reset's
  "Light". Row marks stay per group key (CI-7, CI-9): every Light and Color
  row shares `color`'s mark, and a row's Clear removes the action's whole
  `color` end. No menu item and no shortcut. The chroma key's eyedropper
  neutralises Light with Color while it picks.
- *Wheels* (the Color sub-tab's section after Color, `video.wheels`, §11
  GR-11), closed by default: one row of three columns — Lift, Gamma, Gain —
  each a title, a hue disc in a 1 pt square frame (the column less 4 pt,
  capped at 112 pt: about 78 pt at the default pane), a master strip and a
  master field (2 decimals, no unit, −1…1). The disc is a 5 pt fully saturated
  hue ring, the hue at 35 % saturation fading to the well colour inside, a
  1 pt crosshair and a 7 × 7 square puck at the push, turned so each hue sits
  where the vectorscope draws it (red at 103° counter-clockwise from 3
  o'clock). A press anywhere in the disc and a drag moves the puck by the
  pointer's delta (one disc radius is the full push), ⌥ held at a tenth,
  clamped to the rim; a double-click resets the push (x, y) only. The strip is
  the slider's look with the accent fill from the left end; a press off the
  knob jumps and one on it grabs it where it stands, ⌥ held drags relative at
  a tenth, a double-click resets the master. The push has no typed field
  (exact through MCP); hovering the disc shows "Hue 34° · 0.42" or "Neutral".
  A puck writes only its wheel's `x, y` and a strip or field only its
  `master`, to every selected clip; a drag is one undo step named "Lift",
  "Gamma" or "Gain". Keyed, the pucks and knobs are in action ink with a
  hollow square at the action's start value and a coral mark at the
  playhead's; one row mark and one mixed badge for the whole row. Summary
  "neutral"/"adjusted"; Reset zeroes all nine (undo "Wheels"). No menu item
  and no shortcut.
- *Curves* (the Color sub-tab's section after Wheels, `video.curves`, §11
  GR-12), closed by default: one row — a channel picker (four square text
  cells Master · Red · Green · Blue, the chosen one framed and labelled in its
  channel's tint, a 3 pt dot under each channel that is not the identity; per
  window, Master when a window opens, kept across selection changes, never
  saved), the plot, square, the row less nothing past its 14 pt mark gutter
  and capped at 300 pt (about 254 pt at the default pane, 230 at the 280 pt
  minimum), and under it the selected point's In and Out fields (3 decimals,
  0–1, blank and disabled with no point) and a Delete button. The plot: a
  4 × 4 grid, the identity diagonal, the other channels faint, the edited
  curve in its tint (the strip's own spline, `CurveTable`) and 7 × 7 square
  points, the selected one filled. A press off the points adds a point ON the
  curve at the press's x (on an identity channel at y = x exactly, written
  only once it moves), selects it, and the same press drags it; a drag grabs
  a point where it stands, held 0.001 inside its neighbours' x and inside
  0…1 (the end points too), ⌥ held at a tenth; ⌫/⌦, a double-click on a point
  and Delete remove it (never below 2 points); the arrows nudge it by 0.01
  (⇧ 0.1); Esc deselects; a 17th point beeps. The plot holds first responder
  only while a point is selected, so Space still plays. Every edit writes the
  edited channel's whole list to every selected clip, one undo step named
  "Curves" (a drag the live trio); with an action selected every edit goes to
  its end, a point added or deleted included, and two curves of different
  counts blend over the action (§11 GR-34). Keyed, the curve and its points
  are in action ink with the action's start curve as a 1 pt dashed line; one row
  mark and one mixed badge for the whole row. Summary "neutral"/"adjusted";
  Reset clears all six, a cell's context menu "Reset Red" (etc.) one —
  inside an action each writes the identity curve to the end, so the curve
  animates to neutral. No menu item and no shortcut.
  **The hue curves and the editor's extras (§11 GR-32, grading-4 T4-2).** The
  picker is six equal cells, Master · Red · Green · Blue · Hue · Sat (10 pt
  labels, so "Master" fits its cell at the 230 pt plot). On Hue (Hue vs Hue)
  and Sat (Hue vs Sat) the plot is the square less its bottom 10 pt, where an
  8 pt spectrum strip runs 0° (left) to 360° (right); its grid has verticals
  every 60°, horizontals at the quarters and the neutral line across the
  middle (no change: shift 0°, gain ×1) in place of the diagonal; the faint
  curves behind are only those of the same domain; the identity's two points
  (0° and 180°) are drawn, and a press on an identity hue curve adds its
  point at the neutral. A hue point never crosses 0°/360° (held 0…359.9, 0.1°
  inside each neighbour); the arrows nudge 1° (⇧ 10°) across and 1° or 0.01
  (⇧ 10° or 0.1) up. The In field reads the hue in "°" (1 decimal,
  0–359.9); Out reads the shift in "°" (1 decimal, −180…180) or the gain in
  "×" (2 decimals, 0–2). On every channel, while the pointer is over the plot
  (and during a drag) a readout label (mono 9, the chrome background at 0.85)
  sits at the pointer + (8, 8), flipped inward at the plot's edges: "In 0.42
  → Out 0.47", "Hue 34° → +12°" or "Hue 34° → ×0.80" — the pointer's x and
  the curve's value there; none over the strip. ⇧ held during a drag locks it
  to the axis it first moves along (picked once it has travelled 4 pt; before
  that the point stays put); ⌥ and ⇧ combine.
- *Look* (the Color sub-tab's last section, `video.look`, §11 GR-7), closed by
  default: a 3×3 gallery — None, then the eight looks in the core's order —
  each tile a 256 px thumbnail of the composited frame at the playhead (inside
  the first selected clip, else its first frame) with that tile's click
  applied, its name beneath, and the shared kind outlined in the accent (None
  when no clip has a look, nothing when mixed). Thumbnails ignore Show
  Ungraded and render 0.3 s after the selection, the project or the playhead
  stop changing, only while the section is open. A tile sets the kind on every
  selected clip and keeps each one's amount (1 for a clip with none); None
  removes the look; with an action selected both change the clip's own look.
  Under the grid, when the project holds a `.cube` LUT (§11 GR-8), an
  "Imported" label and a tile per LUT in media-list order, rows of three,
  named by the file's name less `.cube`, outlined when every target's own look
  is that LUT; a missing LUT's tile stays and shows the ungraded frame. Then an
  "Import LUT…" button, off when no selected clip takes a grade: a `.cube`
  chosen in its panel is added to the media list (or reused when it is there)
  and applied to the targets' own looks, one undo step "Import LUT"; a refused
  file shows GR-8's sentence and changes nothing. Under it an Amount row
  (0–100 %, quick 0/25/50/75/100, the one row that writes an action's end)
  when every selected clip has a look. Summary "Warm 80%" (a LUT: "<name>
  80%"), "none" or "mixed" (kinds, LUTs or amounts differ); Reset removes the
  look; undo "Look", and "Amount" for the row. Clip ▸ Look ▸ is its menu (§9).
- *Speed*: rate ×0.1 – ×1000 (§11 #190; the bounds are the core's, read
  through `rc_speed_limits`) with presets (0.5, 1, 2, 4), "Keep pitch", a
  "Fit to" length that sets the rate so the clip plays in it, the note
  "Audio is silent above ×12" (either pitch mode) when a selected clip's
  audio has been left out of the mix (the greatest rate it names) — Rate,
  its presets, Keep pitch, Reset and Fit to first ask, once, before an edit
  takes a clip with sound from at or below ×12 to above it (Set Speed or
  Cancel; Cancel changes nothing and makes no undo step; §11 #190's "The
  question"). With an action selected (§11 SR-21) Rate and Reset write its
  `end.speed`, a speed ramp, read as the end's rate or else the core's
  `clip_state.speed`, keyed `speed` (◆, Clear, the Clip tab's ◆N); Keep
  pitch (at the base rate) and Fit to stay the clip's, ⊘;
  **Reverse**, a "Reversed frames" readout, a Render Reverse button, and
  the resulting duration. Reverse (M3) is a checkbox over `set_reversed`,
  but unlike everything else in this tab it does not change the picture by
  itself: AVFoundation cannot play a clip backwards, so the frames are
  pre-rendered once per media file (§11, §5.6) and the composition reads
  that file instead. Ticking the box therefore starts a render behind a
  progress sheet with a Cancel — and **until the frames exist the clip
  draws the missing-media slate, never forward frames**, because a clip
  that silently played the wrong way was the whole defect the pre-render
  fixes. The readout says which state the clip is in (Rendered, Rendering…,
  Not rendered, Media missing); Render Reverse is live only in the "Not
  rendered" state, which is what a cancelled or failed render leaves
  behind. Nothing retries on its own: a render the user stopped must not
  restart itself on the next keystroke.
- *Actions* is not a section: the **action lane** under the selection
  header, pinned above the scroll view with it, replaces it (§11 CI-1 to CI-4; road map 9 §1). A header row —
  ACTIONS, the count or `none`, "· editing N" in the action ink, and Zoom
  to Region, Zoom Back and Add Action (⌥⌘K); a lane row — the Clip chip,
  active when no action is selected, then the whole clip with its actions
  as bands by fraction, numbered in time order, over the clip's own
  transition hatch, audio fades and motion wedges (§11 CI-12), and the
  playhead; and, only while an action is selected, a detail row — Start
  (clip-relative timecode), Length, a curve glyph and Curve (its titles
  without "Ease "), and Remove. Clicking a band SELECTS the action (and
  parks the playhead at its end), on mouse-UP; dragging its body moves the
  action and dragging its outer 5 pt resizes it, as ONE undo step, with a
  dashed ghost at the original span and the HUD in place of the header
  row's text (§11 CI-11); the Clip chip and esc leave the
  mode. With an action selected, edits in this tab show and set that
  action's END state (§11 CI-2); the per-section note that used to say so
  is gone, and the lane's "editing N" and its wash say it instead. Pan,
  mono, compressor, noise reduction, duck and keep-pitch go to the clip's
  base props whatever is selected (§11 CI-3), and so does one more
  exception, ADDING or REMOVING an entry of a list-valued property
  (`video.filters`, `video.obscure`), which always writes the base props.
  §5.4's list rule interpolates per index only while the two lengths
  match, so a longer or shorter list in an end patch would be invisible
  for the whole span and then be overwritten by the next write of that
  list; dialling a value INSIDE an entry still animates.
- *Transition* appears when the clip overlaps its predecessor: kind,
  curve, whether the two clips' sound crosses over, and duration. The
  Curve popup and the Crossfade Audio checkbox both read the FILE's
  defaults (`linear`, off) rather than what the core would mint — the
  section says what is there — and when the clip has no stored transition
  at all each of the two sends BOTH keys, so the transition that gets
  minted is exactly what the section was showing and only the touched
  value moves.
- M3: *Chroma key* (key color with a canvas eyedropper, tolerance,
  softness, spill suppression), *Reflection*, *Filters* (blur, sharpen,
  pixelate, sepia, noir, vignette, invert — a pass of the one Metal
  compositor, not Core Image; §5.2).
- M3: *Spotlight* — a stand-in for a freehand callout — lives
  here rather than in the Shape pane below, because only the layer that
  owns the pixels can draw its 1–2× zoom (1 is none).
- Format version 4: *Obscure* — up to four regions that hide what is under
  them, six rows each (kind with a Remove button, then shape, amount,
  feather, and the centre and the size as PAIRED fields rather than the
  spotlight's four separate value rows — four regions at eight rows apiece
  is a section nobody can see the end of), with Add Region and "Place on
  Canvas" in the footer, both disabled at the cap. It is the filter
  stack's idiom, not the spotlight's: there is no on/off checkbox because
  an empty list IS off, and the whole array goes back through one
  `set_video` patch. "Place on Canvas" arms §4.2's region mode and
  APPENDS what is drawn — one button for the section rather than one per
  row, because the inspector's command seam carries a selector and no
  index. A stored region is drawn on the canvas with a move grip and
  resize handles while its clip is the one selected (§4.2, §11 CI-16);
  the band only appends.
- Format version 8: *Motion presets* (id `video.motion`; titled *Motion*
  until road map 9's phase 2, on the Clip sub-tab) — In and Out (a kind from the nine
  entrances/exits, or None, each with a duration in seconds), During (a
  kind from the five continuous motions, or None, with Amount % and
  Period — Period disabled for Drift, which ignores it) and a Preview
  button that sets the in/out range to the clip, turns Loop on and plays.
  Shut by default, last on the Clip sub-tab. Every write is one
  `set_video` per clip, so each clip keeps its own other slots, and it
  lands on the base props even inside an action, since motion is not an
  animation target. Also
  Clip ▸ Motion ▸; the timeline's name strip draws the in/out wedges and a
  During glyph. A stored zero-duration In or Out stays selected and editable
  but is explicitly marked inactive in the summary, selection note and menu;
  it draws no wedge (E2-27, §11).

**Audio sections** — clips whose audio is enabled and whose media has an
audio track, or nest clips with audio properties (`InspectorTab.hasAudio`).

- Volume % (0–400, dB shown), Mute, and **Normalize** — one press sets the
  clip's volume so its own loudest moment lands at −1 dBFS, measured from
  the waveform cache over the source window that actually plays. It
  REPLACES the volume rather than multiplying it (so pressing twice changes
  nothing), writes the clip's BASE volume even when the playhead is inside
  an action, leaves `muted` alone, and gives each clip of a selection its
  own gain. A clip quieter than about −13 dBFS cannot reach −1 through the
  model's 4× ceiling: it is normalised as loud as it goes and the result
  says where it landed. Also Clip ▸ Normalize. With an action selected it
  carries a boxed mono "clip" tag: it writes the clip whatever is selected.
- Fade in / fade out in seconds (also the corner handles on the clip).
- Ducking: "lower other audio to N % while this clip plays", with
  attack / release. The rule: for the whole clip extent, not
  voice activity. Realised as volume ramps folded into the other clips'
  envelopes by the core.
- Mix to mono — a fold, not a gain, so it is sample work like the three
  below and lives with them at the head of the tap's Mix stage (§7.2). It
  averages EVERY channel of the composition track and writes the average to
  all of them, and it is inert below two channels. Detach Audio (⌥⌘D; also
  Clip menu).
- Volume actions share the action lane.
- M3 — Pan, "Smooth volume levels" (compressor), "Remove noise" (the
  Noise Reduction section). Unlike everything above, these three — and Mix to mono, which
  joined them in road map 2's phase 5 — cannot be a gain curve, so
  they are not folded into the core's envelope: they are sample work, done
  in Swift by one `MTAudioProcessingTap` per composition audio track, and
  the SAME tap serves the preview and the export (the mix reaches both
  `AVPlayerItem.audioMix` and `AVAssetReaderAudioMixOutput.audioMix`, and
  taps were measured to run on each). A track no clip of which uses them
  gets no tap and is bit-identical to before they existed — on an EXPORT,
  which is where that promise is worth something. A preview build made for
  the level meter (§4.4) taps every audio track, inert ones included,
  because a meter that lights only on the tracks carrying a compressor is
  worse than none; there is no file there to be identical to.
  - Pan is a balance: one gain per channel, constant power across the
    sweep and unity at the centre, so 0 is exactly a bypass and a hard
    pan is +3 dB in the channel it goes to. Inaudible on a clip whose
    source is mono — the widening to stereo happens after the tap. **On a
    clip that also asks for Mix to mono it is a TRUE pan**, because the
    fold runs first and both channels then carry the same signal; that
    ordering is forced rather than chosen, since folding AFTER a pan is
    algebraically a gain change — `(g_L·x + g_R·x)/2 = ((g_L+g_R)/2)·x` —
    and would make the pan control read as broken.
  - The compressor is a channel-strip insert on the track, ahead of the
    fader: its envelope carries across a cut and the volume, fades and
    ducking apply after it. Its attack and release are ticks in the
    schema but sub-frame, and the pane shows them in ms.
  - Noise reduction is a short-time spectral attenuator over a noise
    floor tracked from the clip itself — not a gate, and it delays the
    track it runs on by 512 samples (10.7 ms at 48 kHz).
  - Read at the clip's BASE properties, never through an action, exactly
    as ducking is (§7.2).

**Text sections**

- Content (multi-line), font family / style / size, color, alignment
  (left / center / right and top / middle / bottom within the box), line
  spacing, letter spacing, and an UPPERCASE checkbox in the Style section
  that writes `text.case` (format version 9) — a rendering transform applied
  before layout, so it changes the measured box but never the word count.
- On a word-caption page (a text clip with `word_style`, road map 8), the
  Content field keeps the page's `words` while an edit keeps the word count
  and sends `words: []` in the same `set_text` when it changes it — decided
  PER CLIP, and never `words` inside an action's end patch (§11 W-2, W-6).
  A styled page left untimed that way carries the Content section's
  footnote "Not timed — regenerate to time it again", and the Animation
  section's three rows are greyed with "Word style wins; the reveal does
  not apply." (§11 W-5). Nothing in the inspector writes
  `word_style`: restyling is regenerating (§11 W-10).
- Outline (width, color); Backdrop (fill, opacity, padding, corner
  radius); Shadow (shared section).
- Box: auto-size, or fixed width with wrapping.
- Animation on position / opacity / scale is Actions, like every other
  property. The built-in REVEALS are their own thing and are
  `text.animation` (format version 5): a *Reveal* section (titled
  *Animation* until road map 9's phase 2; the id is unchanged) of three
  controls — a kind popup (None, Typewriter, Word Fade, Line Rise), In and
  Out — written through `set_text` as one whole object, which is the SECTION
  saying all three controls and not a rule about patches: a patch of a nested
  object merges key by key (§5.2, §6), so an agent may send `animation` with
  one key in it and keep the other two. In and Out are DURATIONS measured
  from the clip's own first and last frame, not times.
  The section cannot show the reveal's PROGRESS, and deliberately does not
  try: how far along a reveal is, is a fact about the frame under the
  playhead rather than about the clip, and the core computes it per frame
  into the recipe (§7.3) so the preview, `render_frame` and an export
  cannot disagree about it.
- Presets: Title, Subtitle, Lower third, Caption — set face, size,
  backdrop and position in one click.

**Shape sections**

- Kind: rectangle, ellipse, line, arrow (a rounded rectangle is a
  rectangle with a corner radius). Stroke width
  and color, fill color and opacity, arrowheads at start / end, corner
  radius, Shadow.
- Format version 6: *Gradient* — a Kind popup (Linear, Radial), an Angle
  row disabled for Radial, the stop list as an at-and-colour row apiece
  with Add and Remove, and a Reset that sends `{"gradient": null}` and so
  brings the flat fill back. Every row is DISABLED for `line` and `arrow`,
  which have no area to fill and keep their stroke colour — the field is
  still stored and range-checked on them (§5.2), exactly as `radius` is on
  every kind. Every key of `gradient` is re-sent on every write, which is the
  SECTION knowing all of them rather than a rule about patches: a patch of a
  nested object merges key by key (§5.2, §6), so an agent may send `gradient`
  with one key in it and keep the rest — and a `stops` LIST it sends replaces
  the whole list, which is why the row's Add and Remove re-send every stop.
- M3: *Spotlight* — which darkens the canvas outside a rectangle or
  ellipse, with an optional 1–2× zoom of the region (1 is none) — is a
  `video.spotlight` prop and appears in the **Picture** sub-tab, not here.
