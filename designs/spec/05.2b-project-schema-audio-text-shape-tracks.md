# 5.2b Project — the JSON schema (format version 34): audio, text, shape, transitions, tracks and nests

What a patch means, the example document, the canvas, media, a clip's source and every video field are §5.2a.

- `audio.duck`: `{ "level": 0.2, "attack": 300000, "release": 600000 }`, and
  (format version 29, §11 AU-110..AU-112, AU-116) `mode`, `pre_roll` and
  `hold`, each written only when not its default. `mode` is `extent` (the
  default: every other clip is ducked over this clip's whole length, as
  before 29) or `speech`: only over its media's speech spans
  (`media[].speech`, §5.2a) as the clip plays them — reflected when it is
  reversed, through its rate or speed map — on the part of it that plays,
  each widened to `[start − pre_roll, end + hold]` (300000 each, ≥ 0) and
  merged where they touch. Each widened span ducks as an extent duck over it
  would, and the multiplier is the minimum over the spans. A speech duck
  on a clip that is not a media clip, or whose media has no `speech`, ducks
  nowhere; `set_audio` refuses a patch that sets `mode: "speech"` on one.
  A linked pair's duck belongs to its sound half (§11 AU-157): `detach_audio`
  drops the picture half's, and a linked clip with its picture on and its
  audio off ducks nothing, whatever it carries.
- `audio.fade_in_shape`, `audio.fade_out_shape` (format version 29, §11
  AU-114): `linear` (the default, written only when not), `s_curve`,
  `plus_3db` or `minus_3db` — the fade's gain over its progress u is `u`,
  `(1 − cos πu)/2`, `sin(uπ/2)` or `1 − cos(uπ/2)`. A non-linear fade is
  sampled on the action grid in the plan's ramps (§7.2).
- `audio.pan` (M3) is -1 (hard left) to +1 (hard right);
  `audio.noise_reduction` (M3) is 0 (off) to 1.
- `audio.isolation` (format version 31, §11 AU-50) is 0 (off, the default,
  written only when not 0) to 1: how much of the clip's isolated voice — an
  offline `AUSoundIsolation` render of its window, §7.2 — replaces its own
  sound, as `dry + a·(wet − dry)` before every other stage. `set_audio`
  refuses a value outside 0–1 (`audio.isolation must be between 0 and 1,
  not 1.5`); an action end's value is clamped there and animates it
  (§5.4, §11 AU-85..AU-88), from 0 when the base has none. A time-scaled, ramped or freeze segment plays dry (AU-55); on a
  nest clip it reaches nothing.
- `audio.mono` has been in the schema since M1 and was IGNORED until road
  map 2's phase 5: it is a fold, not a gain, so it belongs with pan and the
  compressor in the tap (§7.2) rather than in the plan, and nothing folded
  it there. It now averages every channel of the composition track into all
  of them, at the head of the Mix stage. No migration and no version bump:
  the key already existed and already defaulted false, so only a project
  that had deliberately set it changes — and changes to what it always
  asked for.
- `audio.compressor` (M3): `null` or `{ "threshold_db": -18, "ratio": 3,
  "attack": 6000, "release": 120000, "makeup_db": 0 }` — "smooth volume
  levels". Unlike ducking, this cannot be expressed as a gain curve, so
  the model carries the parameters and the DSP is Swift's.
- `audio.limiter` (format version 30, §11 AU-20..AU-23): absent (off) or
  `{ "ceiling_db": -1, "release": 60000 }` — a true-peak limiter, last in
  the clip's chain. `ceiling_db` is dBTP AFTER the clip's base volume
  (−24…0); `release` is ticks (600…600 000, 1 ms…1 s). Written only when
  set, so a project without one writes the text it did; `{}` stores the
  defaults, `null` clears it, out of range is refused by its dotted path and
  an action's end is clamped to the ranges and switches it at the end
  (§5.4); a nest clip's reaches nothing. The DSP is Swift's
  (`AudioLimiterStage`), told the base volume (§11 AU-22).
- `audio.channels` (format version 32, §11 AU-34): `"both"` (the default,
  not written), `"left"`, `"right"` or `"swapped"` — the chain's head on a
  source of two or more channels: `left` and `right` copy that channel into
  every channel, `swapped` exchanges them; a mono track ignores it, as it
  ignores pan. A word the vocabulary lacks is refused.
- `audio.eq` (format version 32, §11 AU-30, AU-31): absent (off) or
  `{ "high_pass", "low_shelf", "band_1", "band_2", "band_3", "high_shelf",
  "low_pass", "bypass" }`, each slot absent (off) or an object: a pass
  `{ "frequency", "slope" }` (Hz 20…20 000; 12 or 24 dB/octave, one or two
  Butterworth sections; high-pass `{80, 12}`, low-pass `{12000, 12}`), a
  shelf `{ "frequency", "gain_db" }` (dB −24…24; low `{100, 0}`, high
  `{8000, 0}`), a bell `{ "frequency", "gain_db", "q" }` (Q 0.1…10;
  `{1000, 0, 1}`); `bypass` (written only when true) keeps the settings and
  silences them. After the pick, before noise reduction.
- `audio.hum` (format version 32, §11 AU-33): absent (off) or
  `{ "frequency": 60, "depth_db": 30, "harmonics": 4 }` — notches at the
  mains (50 or 60 Hz) and its harmonics (1…8), each `depth_db` (6…60) deep;
  after the EQ. For all three: written only when set, so a project without
  them writes the text it did; `{}` stores a group's or a slot's defaults,
  `null` clears one, out of range is refused by its dotted path, an action's
  end is clamped to the ranges and animates them (§5.4; a slope, the mains
  and the harmonics switch at the end, §11 AU-86); a nest clip's reach
  nothing. The DSP is Swift's
  (`AudioEQStage`, §11 AU-30) (§11 AU-37).
- `audio.gate` (format version 33, §11 AU-40, AU-43): absent (off) or
  `{ "threshold_db": -10, "ratio": 4, "range_db": 20, "attack": 600,
  "hold": 30000, "release": 60000 }` — a downward expander after noise
  reduction: `threshold_db` (−40…0) is dB RELATIVE to the clip's measured
  loudness (§11 AU-41), below it the level drops `ratio − 1` dB per dB
  (ratio 1…20), never more than `range_db` (1…80); `attack` (0…60 000
  ticks) opens it, `hold` (0…300 000) keeps it open, `release` (600…1 200
  000) closes it.
- `audio.de_esser` (format version 33, §11 AU-44, AU-45): absent (off) or
  `{ "frequency": 6000, "threshold_db": 0, "range_db": 6 }` — after the
  gate, the band above `frequency` (2 000…12 000 Hz, a Linkwitz–Riley
  crossover) turned down as far as its sidechain passes `threshold_db`
  (−30…+20, relative to the clip's loudness), never more than `range_db`
  (1…24). For both: written only when set, after `channels`; `{}` stores
  the defaults, `null` clears, out of range is refused by its dotted path,
  an action's end is clamped and animates them (§5.4); a nest clip's reach
  nothing. Until the clip's loudness is measured neither acts,
  and an export waits for it (§11 AU-42) (§11 AU-46).
- `audio.leveller` (format version 37, §11 AU-66): absent (off) or
  `{ "target_db": 0, "max_up_db": 12, "max_down_db": 12, "response":
  1200000 }` — after the gate, a gain curve made ahead of time from the
  clip's measured loudness and its speech: each phrase's level over a 3 s
  window of its speech, smoothed forward and back over `response`
  (300 000…6 000 000 ticks), is brought to `target_db` (−20…10, relative to
  the clip's loudness), never more than `max_up_db` up or `max_down_db` down
  (each 0…24); a pause holds and glides to the next phrase over its last
  0.25 s. Written only when set, after `voice`; `{}` stores the defaults,
  `null` clears, out of range is refused by its dotted path, an action's end
  is clamped and animates the target and the limits while `response`
  switches at the end (§5.4); a nest clip's reaches nothing; until the clip's
  loudness and speech are measured it does not act, and an export waits;
  without speech spans where speech detection is unavailable it does not act
  (§11 AU-66 (e)).
- `audio.voice` (format version 34, §11 AU-140): absent (off) or
  `{ "kind": "hall", "amount": 1, "pitch": 5 }` — a voice effect, written
  after `de_esser` and only when set. `kind` is one of `pitch`, `high`,
  `deep`, `robot`, `bass`, `treble`, `bright`, `telephone`, `radio`, `lofi`,
  `distorted`, `echo`, `room`, `hall`, `plate`, `cathedral` and has no
  default; `amount` 0…1 (default 1) scales the whole chain; `pitch` is
  semitones −12…12, written only when set, read only by `pitch`, `high` and
  `deep`, and absent means the kind's own (0, +5, −5). `null` clears it, a
  patch merges key by key (`pitch: null` drops the stored pitch), a patch with
  no `kind` over no voice is refused in serde's sentence, out of range is
  refused by its dotted path (`audio.voice.amount must be between 0 and 1,
  not 1.5`), and an action's end is clamped and ignored. Base-only; a nest
  clip's reaches nothing. The render is Swift's, offline (§7.2, AU-141,
  AU-142); a time-scaled, ramped or freeze segment plays without it (AU-145).
- `text`: `{ "content": "…", "font": "SourceSans3-SemiBold", "size": 72, "color": [...],
  "align": "center", "valign": "middle", "line_spacing": 1.0, "tracking": 0,
  "outline": null | { "width": 2, "color": [...] },
  "backdrop": null | { "color": [...], "padding": 12, "radius": 8 },
  "width": null | 800,
  "animation": null | { "kind": "typewriter", "in": 600000, "out": 0 },
  "case": "as_written" | "upper",
  "words": [] | [{ "start": 0, "end": 108000 }, …],
  "word_style": null | { "mode": "highlight" | "build",
    "active_color": null | [...], "active_scale": 1.0,
    "active_pill": null | { "color": [...], "padding": 0.2, "radius": 0.25,
      "glide": false },
    "spoken_color": null | [...], "upcoming_color": null | [...],
    "active_fill": false, "pause_dim": 0.0 } }` —
  `word_style` closes the object.
- `text.animation` (format version 5): how the words ARRIVE and how they
  LEAVE, or `null` — the common case — for text that is simply there for
  the length of its clip. `kind` ∈ `typewriter`, `word_fade`, `line_rise`,
  a closed vocabulary like `video.obscure`'s `kind` and refused on the way
  in if it is anything else, because each one is a different drawing
  routine and there is no neutral reveal to fall back to. `in` and `out`
  are DURATIONS, not times: the reveal runs for `in` from the clip's own
  first frame and the un-reveal for `out` back from its own last one,
  either of them 0 for "that end does not happen". An animation that says
  nothing is a one-second typewriter that does not un-reveal.
  Both halves measure against the CLIP's own duration, never the visible
  span of the instance drawing it, which is the rule clip time already
  follows (§7.3) — a nest clip's edge cuts a nested caption off mid-reveal
  rather than making it un-reveal early. `in + out` longer than the clip
  does not refuse: the reveal is the smaller of the two ramps, so a short
  clip shows a partial reveal — and keeps ONE phase for the whole of its
  life, which §7.3 argues — and nothing has to be clamped to a length a
  later trim could change. There is deliberately no curve field — the
  shape of the motion is in the kind, and easing between two arbitrary
  values is what actions are for (§5.4) — and no per-frame number: the
  core computes that into the recipe (§7.3), so the model says what the
  animation IS and nothing about where it has got to.
- `text.case`, `text.words` and `text.word_style` (format version 9, road
  map 8's word captions; §11 W-1 to W-6): a word caption is an ordinary
  text clip holding one PAGE. `case` is a transform applied before layout
  (`as_written`, the default, or `upper`); it changes the measured width
  and never the token count. `words` is `[]` for an untimed text, or ONE
  `{start, end}` per whitespace token of `content` (`str::split_whitespace`
  — the core is the one counter), in CLIP ticks: a negative start is legal
  and means "already said", two words may share a start (the later one
  wins that frame), starts never decrease and an `end` is never before its
  `start`. Every word time is snapped to the frame grid like any tick a
  patch carries, and stays inside D7-1's envelope. `word_style` is `null`
  or how the word being said is drawn: `mode` `highlight` (the whole page,
  the active word picked out) or `build` (words appear as they are said);
  the three colours are `null` for "the text's own colour"; `active_scale`
  (1.0–1.5) is where the active word's pop settles; `active_pill`'s
  `padding` and `radius` are FRACTIONS of `size`. All three keys default,
  so a version-8 file reads unchanged; the per-frame answer is computed
  into the recipe (§7.3), never stored.
- `word_style`'s three looks (format version 12, issue batch B86; §11 W-12
  to W-14), each a key and each off by default, so a version-11 page draws
  as it did: `active_pill.glide` (`false`), the pill SLIDES from the word
  before to the active one instead of jumping; `active_fill` (`false`), the
  active word fills left to right from its `start` to its `end`, in 0.6 s
  at most; `pause_dim` (0.0 off, to 1.0 invisible), the page's opacity in a
  pause of 0.25 s or more between one word's `end` and the next one's
  `start` is `1 − pause_dim`. `glide` closes the pill, and `active_fill`
  then `pause_dim` close the style. They compose with each other and with
  both modes. How far each has got at an instant is computed into the
  recipe (§7.3: `word_glide`, `word_fill`, `word_dim`), never stored.
- `shape`: `{ "kind": "arrow", "width": 320, "height": 120,
  "stroke": { "width": 6, "color": [...] }, "fill": null | [...],
  "arrow_start": false, "arrow_end": true, "radius": 0,
  "from": [0, 1], "to": [1, 0], "gradient": null }` — `from`/`to` are unit
  coordinates in the box, for line and arrow only.
- `shape.gradient` (format version 6): `null` — the common case — or
  `{ "kind": "linear" | "radial", "stops": [{ "at": 0..1,
  "color": [r,g,b,a] }, …], "angle": 0 }`, drawn by
  `LayerRenderers.renderShape` with `CGGradient` and taking precedence
  over `fill` while it is non-null. **Setting one does not clear `fill`**:
  both persist, so `{"gradient": null}` is an exact inverse — the
  section's Reset brings the colour back out from under it — and the
  precedence rule stays in one statable place instead of becoming a side
  effect of whichever op wrote last. Between **2 and 8 stops**
  (`MAX_GRADIENT_STOPS`), named rather than trimmed: one stop is a solid
  and `fill` already spells that, and the ceiling is 8 where
  `video.filters`' and `video.obscure`'s are 4 because those two are
  SHADER SLOTS — the uniform block carries exactly four of each — while a
  gradient is drawn on the CPU, where nothing downstream has slots at all.
  `angle` is degrees from the RIGHT, counter-clockwise (`video.shadow`'s
  convention, the crate's only one); it is meaningless for `radial` and is
  stored and range-checked there anyway, so flipping the kind and back
  does not lose it. `{"gradient": {}}` is the type's own default — linear,
  angle 0, black at 0 to white at 1 — which is the feature's first
  impression and is what the inspector's Add sends.
  **The stops are deliberately NOT checked for order.** `CGGradient`
  requires monotonic locations or its output is undefined, so a refusal
  looks like the tidy answer; it has a failure mode nobody would accept,
  because dragging one stop past another is how a gradient is edited and
  the drag would stop working half way through. `video.filters` is the
  precedent — an ordered list the core does not police — and the fix
  belongs where the requirement is, so the RENDERER sorts before it draws.
  The question is a RENDER-time one and only that: `shape` is not among
  the groups an action's end patch may carry (§5.4), so no gradient is ever
  interpolated and an unsorted stop list is only ever drawn.
  `line` and `arrow` have no area and IGNORE a gradient entirely — stored,
  range-checked, not drawn, with the inspector's rows disabled for them,
  which is exactly `shape.radius`'s precedent (every kind carries it, only
  the rectangle reads it). Refusing it per kind would mean the kind popup
  had to clear the field on its way past, and a value a popup deleted is
  work nobody can get back.
- `actions[].end` is a patch: any subset of `video`, `audio`, `text` keys.
  `curve` ∈ `linear`, `ease_in`, `ease_out`, `ease_in_out`.
- `transition_in`: `null` or `{ "kind": "cross_dissolve" | "fade_black" |
  "fade_white" | "push_left" | "push_right" | "push_up" | "push_down" |
  "wipe" | "slide" | "zoom" | "wipe_left" | "wipe_up" | "wipe_down",
  "curve": curve, "audio": bool, "audio_shape": shape }`. Its duration *is* the overlap with the
  previous clip on the track; a clip with no overlap ignores it. `wipe` is
  the original rightward one and the three directions beside it are
  **version 7**'s, appended; `curve` (version 7, default `linear`) eases the
  progress the compositor is handed, and `audio` (version 7, default
  `false`) crossfades the two clips' sound across the overlap at constant
  power. Both defaults are the FILE's and deliberately differ from what the
  core MINTS — every transition an edit makes from version 7 on says
  `ease_in_out` and `audio: true` (§11, R5-2), so a project written before
  the keys existed looks and sounds exactly as it did. `audio_shape`
  (format version 29, §11 AU-115) is the crossfade's curve over the eased
  progress p, one of `audio.fade_in_shape`'s four: the incoming clip gets
  `gain(p)` and the outgoing `gain(1 − p)`. Its default — file and minted —
  is `plus_3db`, R5-2's constant power bit for bit, written only when not.
- `name` (format version 3): the clip's own name, or `null` — the common
  case — meaning "call it what it shows": the app falls back to the nest's
  name for a nest clip, the media file's name where there is one, else the
  kind (`ProjectSnapshot.name(of:)`, the one fallback the name strip, the
  selection block and the Clip tab header all read). `set_clip` stores it
  trimmed; the schema keeps whatever it is given.
- `label` (format version 3): `null`, or a colour NAME from the marker
  vocabulary — `red`, `orange`, `yellow`, `green`, `blue`, `purple`, the
  one list, spelled once on the app side (`ClipLabel.swift`) and drawn as a
  6 pt dot on the clip (§4.4). Stored unvalidated, exactly like a marker's
  `color` and for the same reason: a build that policed the list would
  refuse a project written by a newer one, so an unknown name is kept and
  drawn in the neutral ink.
- `tracks[].solo` (format version 3): while any track in a container's
  track set — the timeline's, or one nest's own — is soloed, every track
  in that set without `solo` is treated as muted; mute wins, so a track
  that is both muted and soloed stays silent. Audio only — it hides
  nothing — and per container, so a nest's own tracks are judged among
  themselves. The rule is on the set, not on what plays: a soloed track
  with nothing audible on it still silences the rest (§7.2).
- `tracks[].matte` (format version 14, §11 MT-1): `"alpha"`,
  `"alpha_inverted"`, `"luma"` or `"luma_inverted"` makes the track a
  matte for the track directly behind it in its container — its clips are
  never drawn on the canvas, and what they show cuts that track (MT-2,
  MT-3). Written ONLY when it is not `"none"`, the model's one key with no
  default spelled out in the file, so an ordinary track and every older
  file round-trip byte for byte; an absent key reads `"none"`. After
  `solo`. Its clips still sound, and `set_track` is its one writer.
- `tracks[].volume` and `tracks[].pan` (format version 35, §11 AU-102):
  the track's fader, linear 0…4 (+12.04 dB), default 1, and its pan, −1
  (left) … 1 (right), default 0, each written only when not its default,
  after `matte`, on the timeline's tracks and a nest's alike. Volume
  multiplies every clip's envelope on the track after its mute (§7.2,
  AU-103); pan is a constant-power balance after each clip's own pan, carried
  in the plan (AU-104), and has no effect on a mono source. A nested clip
  hears its own track's and every enclosing nest clip's track's. `set_track`
  is their one writer.
- `tracks[].compressor` and `tracks[].limiter` (format version 36, §11
  AU-150, AU-151): the clip's own `audio.compressor` and `audio.limiter`
  objects, each written only when set, after `pan`. They run post-fader on
  the track's sub-mix — after every clip's chain, the envelope (the track's
  volume included) and the track's pan — compressor then limiter, the
  ceiling in dBTP on what the track hands the mix; a nest's whole mix passes
  through the track holding its nest clip, which is its bus. Timeline tracks
  only: a nest's own tracks have none, and one written there by hand is
  ignored where it plays. Exact in every export (per-track sub-mixes, §7.8);
  approximate in the preview (§11 AU-153). `set_track` is their one writer.
- `nests` (M3) holds the groups every `nest` clip points at:
  `{ "id": 60, "name": "Intro", "width": 1920, "height": 1080,
  "tracks": [...], "clips": [...] }`. A nest is **not** a nested project:
  its tracks and clips live in the parent's one id space, reference the
  parent's `media[]`, and start from 0 — so the plan's clip-keyed track
  map, `CompositionBuilder`'s media lookup and `next_id` all keep working
  when a nest is flattened at evaluation time. Its `width`/`height` are
  frozen at the size the parent had when it was made, so a later canvas
  change does not silently re-lay-out a nest. A nest's duration is the
  largest clip end inside it; the nest CLIP's duration is independent and
  trims it. Nesting is capped at **8** levels, a nest may not reach
  itself, and nested tracks and clips count toward the same caps as the
  timeline's.
- Ids are unique across all kinds in one project (`next_id`) — INCLUDING
  everything inside a nest — so an MCP call can name anything by one
  integer.
- **Not** in the model: selection, playhead, zoom, scroll, panel sizes.
  That is view state and lives in Swift.
