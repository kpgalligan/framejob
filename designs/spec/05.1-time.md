# 5.1 Time

Integer ticks at **600 000 per second** (`Ticks = i64`, `TIMESCALE =
600_000`) — a timescale that divides every common frame duration exactly:
24, 25, 30, 48, 50, 60, 120 fps and the 24000/1001 and 30000/1001 rates
(a 29.97 frame is 20 020 ticks, a 23.976 frame 25 025). `CMTime` at
timescale 600 000 round-trips exactly. Edits snap to the document's frame
grid (`rc_time_snap_to_frame`, round-to-nearest); sources keep their own
rate and AVFoundation picks the source frame. Frame rates are rationals
`[num, den]` everywhere.

Structural stored times use D7-1's inclusive ±9,000,000,000,000,000-tick
envelope: media duration, marker time, every top-level or nested clip's
start, duration and source `in`/`at`, every action's start and duration, and
clip/action endpoints computed in i128. Positive clip/action duration and
nonnegative clip start retain their earlier checks and wording. Property
vocabulary times and finite positive speeds are not newly bounded. Rational
snapping keeps a wide frame index until its public result is saturated.
This envelope is below 2^53 and leaves Int64 headroom for app timeline
sums. `segment_of` clamps media ranges against admitted media duration and
source position before `CompositionBuilder` reads them; a still's full-u32
rational frame is at most `600000 × u32::MAX` ticks. Those bounds keep
`sourceIn + sourceDuration` within Int64 without a duplicate Swift policy.

A frames timecode `HH:MM:SS:FF` (`format_timecode`, `parse_timecode`) is
non-drop at an NTSC rate — one whose denominator over the gcd of numerator
and denominator is 1001 — counting `ceil(num/den)` frames to a second
(§7.3b). At every other fractional rate it counts real seconds (§11 #532):
SS is the whole second the frame starts in, `floor(index·den/num)`, and FF
the frames since that second's first frame, `index − ceil(SS·num/den)`, so a
12.5 fps second holds 13 frames then 12. A whole rate reads the same either
way.
