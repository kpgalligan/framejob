# 7.9f Agent — the timeline transcript

The app-state tools are §7.9e; `get_transcript`, one clip's words, is among them. This file holds the tool that reads the whole edited container (road map "creator-jobs" TX1, §11 TX-10…TX-14).

`get_timeline_transcript {from?, to?, tracks?, language?}` — every heard word
of the edited container (the nest `set_scope` has open, else the timeline) in
timeline order, nested instances included. An app-state read with no `scope`
key: it runs off the main thread, the assistant's Stop reaches its fill, it
never makes a network request, it needs no key (TX-14), it has no AI-7 row
and it makes no undo step. It is the hundred-and-ninth product tool.

**What is read** (TX-10). The instances are the composition plan's audio
segments (§7.2): a nested segment carries `path` and `origin`. An instance is
read when its inner clip passes `Transcriber.jobs`' gate and its track's
envelope is not zero throughout the segment, so a muted or soloed-out clip,
or one at volume 0, gives no words. Each media's words come from its cached
AssemblyAI transcript when its marker serves captions, else from this Mac's
recogniser (the fill, below); they are placed by R6-10's mapping (through the
source map for a ramp), and a nested word is moved by the segment's `origin`
less the inner clip's start and kept only inside the segment.

**The answer.**
- `words[]`: `text`, `start`, `end` (timeline ticks), `clip` (the inner
  clip), `track` (the container track it plays on; a nested word's is its
  enclosing nest clip's), `media`, `engine` (`apple` or `assemblyai`),
  `filler` (TR-8), `speaker` when known, `editable`, `why` when not editable
  (TX-11: inside a nest, under a transition, on a locked track, in that order,
  each in its sentence), and `id {nest_path, media, source, k}`. Ordered by
  start, end, front track first, source order. `k` counts the earlier words
  with the same nest path, media and source tick over the whole container,
  before the window, `tracks` and paging, so it is 0 unless one source plays
  twice; the inner clip's id is left out because a ripple renumbers a split's
  second half. `cut_start` and `cut_end` (road map "creator-jobs" TX3a, §11
  TX-30, TX-33): the range the Transcript tab cuts that word by when it alone
  is deleted — its onset to the next word's onset (at most 1 s past its end),
  widened over any unlocked word of `tracks` it would cut into, snapped (§4.3e)
  — computed over every read word of `tracks` before the window and paging,
  against the container as the call read it, each needed media's voice cache
  read on the server thread. A run of words is cut with one
  `ripple_delete_ranges` range from its first word's `cut_start` to its last
  word's `cut_end`.
- `media[]`: each media read, `{media, engine, language, fillers_reported}`.
- `no_words[]`: `{clip, nest_path, tag, reason}` for every media clip the
  gate refuses, found by walking the container's clips and every nest they
  show, unwindowed; `tag` "clip 12" or "clip 12 in nest Intro".
- `notes[]`: one per pair of clips of different media that overlap for at
  least 5 s with matching words ("Clips 3 and 7 carry the same speech. Mute
  one, or switch its audio off, to read it once."), and TR-11's filler
  sentence once when a media's transcript did not ask for fillers.
- `count`, `project`; `from`/`to` echoed when given; `truncated: true` and
  `next_from` when the answer was paged.

**The window and `tracks`.** `from`/`to` are TR-13's: snapped to frames,
`to` not after `from` refused ("to must be after from: …"), clamped to the
container's `[0, duration)`, and refused when nothing is left ("The window
from … is outside the timeline, which runs from 0 to … ticks."); a word
across an edge is answered whole. `tracks` keeps the words on those
container tracks; an unknown id is refused by `noSuchId`'s sentence. The
same-speech notes are judged over the windowed words before `tracks`.

**Paging.** At most 2,000 words a page. When more follow, the page is cut
back until the next word's frame floor is past the last kept word's, and
`next_from` is that floor (the largest frame boundary at or before the next
word's start), which `from`'s snap returns unchanged; a cut that would empty
the page keeps it whole and `next_from` is the boundary after its last word's
frame. Passing `next_from` back drops no word; a word overlapping it may come
back with the same `id`.

**The fill** (TX-12). Each heard media in the window with no cloud transcript
is looked up in `cache/device-transcripts/` (§5.6) as ONE whole-file run;
a miss is read by the document's `TranscriptFill`: owners want items, one
media at a time on a serial `.userInitiated` queue, the media under the
playhead first, a whole-file `Transcriber.Job` through
`TranscriptReader.recognize`, stored only when not cancelled and the file did
not change. Stop withdraws only this call's want; a run no other owner wants
is cancelled, answered "The transcription was cancelled.", and stores
nothing. A failed run refuses the call with the recogniser's sentence. The
language is this Mac's unless `language` names another (refused, by
`transcribe`'s two sentences, when unknown or not installed — only when a
media needs this Mac's words). TX-S1 measured an hour of `say` speech in
40.8 s on an M2 Max with `play_preview` composing every frame.
