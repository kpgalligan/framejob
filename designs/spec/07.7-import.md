# 7.7 Import

`MediaImporter.probe(url)` (async): loads `duration`, video/audio tracks,
`naturalSize` × `preferredTransform` (so rotated phone footage reports its
display size), `nominalFrameRate` (converted to the nearest rational
among the common rates, else `[round(fps × 1000), 1000]`), alpha
(`hasAlphaChannel` on the format description), and returns the `media[]`
descriptor for `add_media` — and for `relink_media`'s `probe`, so a relink
takes the new file's duration, size, rate and flags (§11, #63). A video
track must also decode: the probe reads its first frame to 32BGRA through an
`AVAssetReader` (`DecoderSupport.decodesFirstFrame`) and refuses the file as
`undecodable`, in a sentence naming the codec, only when the reader fails
with "no decoder" (-11833) or "undecodable media data" (-11855); damaged data
in a codec the Mac decodes imports as before, and the refusal is never
offered to ffmpeg (§11, #216). Every process opts in to macOS's supplemental
VP9 and AV1 decoders first (`DecoderSupport.enable()`: at launch, before a
cache pass, before a composition looks up a track).
Thumbnails: 160 px tall, one per second up to 600, JPEG in
`cache/thumbs`. Peaks: mono float, 100 buckets/s, in
`cache/peaks`. Both run in the background after import and the timeline
redraws as they land — and both are SKIPPED when the cache's completion
marker matches the file (§5.6); a relinked, changed, partial or unmarked
cache is rebuilt. Image files (PNG/JPEG/HEIC) are probed with ImageIO
and become `kind: image` media. **On the grading branch** (§11 GR-8) a
`.cube` file (by its extension, any case) is read whole by the core's parser
(`rc_cube_parse`, at most `RC_CUBE_MAX_BYTES` + 1 bytes read) and becomes
`kind: lut` media — 0×0, duration 0, no flags, no caches — or is refused as
undecodable in the parser's words (`“Film.cube” is not a LUT Reelcut reads:
line 2: LUT_3D_SIZE 200 is outside 2–65.`), never offered to ffmpeg. File ▸
Add Media… lists `.cube`; a Finder drop on the Media tab imports one; a drop
on the window or the Dock does not (those open documents).

Formats measured on 2026-10-02 on macOS 26.5.1 (25F80), Apple M2 Max
(`hw_av1` false, `hw_vp9` true after the opt-in, `hw_hevc` true), by
`tools/proof/formats_probe.py` — the product's own `MediaImporter.waitForProbe`
(no ffmpeg rewrap) and one decode of the first frame, sound buffer or image,
over 1 s, 128×72 ffmpeg-made files. **Opens** (probe and decode): MOV, MP4,
M4V, MPEG-TS and AVI containers; H.264, HEVC (`hvc1`), ProRes 422, MJPEG and
MPEG-4 Part 2 video; VP9 Profile 0 and Profile 2 (10-bit) in MP4; M4A (AAC
and ALAC), ADTS AAC, MP3, WAV, AIFF, CAF, FLAC, Ogg Opus and Ogg Vorbis audio.
**Images** (ImageIO): PNG, JPEG, TIFF (RGB), GIF, BMP and HEIC open.
**Refused as undecodable** (§11, #216): AV1 in MP4 ("mp4_av1.mp4 is AV1
video, which needs an M3 or later Mac.", `isPlayable` false) and VP9 Profile
1 (4:4:4) in MP4 ("mp4_vp9_p1.mp4 is VP9 Profile 1 video, which this Mac
cannot decode; VP9 Profiles 0 and 2 import.", though `isPlayable` is true).
**Refused** (the probe throws `unreadable`): MKV (H.264, AV1) and WebM (VP9
with Opus, VP8 with Vorbis) (§11). **Probe-only**: a 640×360 H.264 MP4 whose
first GOP was overwritten with 2048 random bytes imports, and its first frame
does not decode ("Cannot Decode", -11821). VP8 in MP4 was not made (ffmpeg
wrote no packets). VP9 and AV1 in MOV were not measured: ffmpeg will not
write either into `.mov`. AV1 decode depends on hardware, and a host with an
AV1 hardware decoder (M3 or later) may answer differently. WebP was not
measured (no encoder on the host).

**WebM and Matroska open by a built-in rewrap** (§11, #216-R; issue batch
B73). When AVFoundation refuses a file whose first four bytes are an EBML
header (1A 45 DF A3), the import funnel (`MediaImporter.probeAllowingRewrap`)
hands it to `ContainerRewrap.assist` with no question: the core indexes the
container (`ReelContainer` over `rc_container_*`) and `AVAssetWriter` copies
its first video and first audio stream, unchanged, into
`~/Library/Application Support/Reelcut/Rewrapped/<stem>-<16 hex>.mov` — the
folder and the digest name ffmpeg's conversions use, so a second import
reuses the copy — and the copy is probed as any movie is. **Copied:** VP9
(with a `vpcC` built from the first keyframe), AV1, H.264 and HEVC video;
Opus (OpusHead as the cookie, each packet's length from its TOC), Vorbis
(§11 #228-V: `vorb` with the CodecPrivate's three Xiph-laced headers as the
cookie, each packet's count by the overlap rule — prev/4 + cur/4 from its
mode's block size, packet 0 bs0/4 + its own/4 — and every packet placed
packet 0's count later, the lead, because packet 0 decodes to nothing) and
AAC (an ES_Descriptor cookie) sound. **Before anything is written:** VP8,
AV1 without hardware decode and VP9 Profile 1/3 are refused in #216's
sentences; a codec the copy does not carry (FLAC, MP3, AC-3, MPEG-2 and the
rest, a Vorbis track whose headers cannot be read, or a compressed or
encrypted track) is refused as "`<file>` has FLAC audio, which Reelcut
cannot copy out of a WebM or Matroska file." and
alone falls through to the opt-in ffmpeg step — which a tool call never asks
(§11 MC-5), so its refusal then also names `RCFFmpegImport`, as an
AVFoundation refusal's does; the core's `required_bytes`
(the copied payload, 64 bytes a sample and 16 MiB) is compared with the
destination volume's free space for important usage, and a copy that will
not fit is refused as "Not enough free space to open `<file>`: its video and
sound need about `<size>` on “`<volume>`”, which has `<size>` free. Nothing
was copied." **Timing:** video lying within a quarter frame of its rate's
grid is written on it (frame k at k·den in a timescale of num), so an
exact-time request lands on the file's own frame; a variable-rate file keeps
its millisecond times. The movie's own timescale — the grid of each track's
edit and duration — is the least common multiple of 600 and its tracks'
timescales (48000 for 30 fps video with 48 kHz sound, 240000 at 30000/1001),
falling back to 600 and the sound's alone and then to the writer's default
when the movie would not fit 31 bits, so a track's start is not rounded to
1/600 s (§11 #232; `container_rewrap_probe.py` G24, G25); a copy made before
that is reused as it is. **A failure** leaves no file (the copy is written as
`<stem>.partial.mov` and moved into place) and says "Could not copy
`<file>` into a movie: `<reason>`." A copy #216's one-frame decode still
refuses is deleted and refused in the source's name. Dropped: every track
after the first of each kind, subtitles, chapters, attachments and VP9
alpha. Measured by `tools/proof/container_rewrap_probe.py` and
`tools/proof/container_import_proof.py` (the proof README's B73 section).

**A person's copy shows its progress, and can be cancelled** (§11 #228-P).
Every click door — File ▸ Add Media…, a drop into a project, Relink from the
Media tab or the missing-media sheet, and File ▸ Open, a Finder double-click
or a drop on the Dock or the welcome window — hands the copy a watch
(`ProjectDocument.copyWatch`, `ProjectDocument.probeForOpening`), and a copy
still running 1 s after it starts shows a small non-modal window titled
"Copying Movie": "Copying “`<file>`”", a bar, "Reading the file…" until the
copy begins and then "`<copied>` of `<total>`", and a Cancel button
(`RewrapProgressWindowController`). The window never takes the keyboard
from a Reelcut window — it is made key only when no window is — so Escape
cancels only once the person has clicked into it. Cancel reads
"Cancelling…"; the copy stops before its next sample, its `.partial.mov` is
deleted, the file is left out with no alert, and the other files of the
import still land; File ▸ Open of it opens nothing. A re-import reuses a
finished copy with no window. **A tool call passes no watch**
(`import_media`, `relink_media`, `record_screen`, and `open_project`, which
reads off the main thread, on the agent lane's thread inside
`MediaImporter.withoutAsking`, §11 #231): no window, the same wait and the
same answer, and no progress message or cancel over MCP (§11 MC-5). Measured
by `container_rewrap_probe.py` C20–C30 (the proof README's B76 section).

**A copy remembers its original** (§11 #228-O, format version 11). The
built-in copy records the WebM or Matroska file it was made from as the
media's `original` (`MediaImporter.probeCopy`); `path` and `bookmark` stay
the copy's. The media is named after the original everywhere a name is shown
(`MediaSnapshot.name`), and the Media tab row's tooltip says where the copy
lives. Reveal in Finder opens the original, or the copy when the original is
gone. A relink to another WebM or Matroska file is copied as an import is
and records the new original; a relink to any other file clears it.
Consolidate copies the `.mov` into the package and keeps the original, so
the name stays. **A copy that is gone is copied again on open**
(`ProjectDocument+MissingCopies.swift`, after the consolidated re-anchor): a
media whose copy resolves nothing, or resolves inside the Trash (where
Finder's delete puts it), and whose original is still there and still a
Matroska file is copied again by the built-in copy alone — never ffmpeg,
never a question — and relinked to it, with no undo step and no alert. A
person's open shows #228-P's window; a Cancel or a failure leaves that media
missing (the slate, the badge, Relink…), with no alert, and the next open
tries again. **Tool calls:** an agent's `open_project` reads off the main
thread (§11 #231): its re-copy shows no window and asks nothing (MC-5), every
window stays usable for the copy's length, other tool calls wait for it, and
it answers when the read ends (`agent_open_proof.py` A0–A7 and
`container_import_proof.py` M12, the proof README's B79 section); while a
person's open of the same file is still reading, or begins during the
agent's read, the two end as one document — the call waits for the person's
open and answers `already_open`, or is refused by name after 30 s — and
`new_project` and `save_project` refuse a path being opened (§11 #234;
`agent_open_proof.py` A8–A14, the proof README's B81 section);
`import_media` and `relink_media`
answer the original's path as `original`, `add_media`'s descriptor takes
`original {path, bookmark?}`, and `get_project` carries it as the model's
own key. A file the opt-in ffmpeg step converts records its original the
same way (§11 #233; `MediaImporter.probeConverted`), on every door, so its
name, tooltip, Reveal in Finder and the tools' `original` are the same; the
re-copy on open stays the built-in copy's alone, so a missing ffmpeg-made
conversion stays missing until a Relink. Measured by
`container_rewrap_probe.py` O31–O43 and `container_import_proof.py` M8–M11
(the proof README's B78 section), and for a conversion by
`ffmpeg_rewrap_proof.py` O1–O5 (its B80 section).

**The opt-in ffmpeg step copies a constant-rate VP9 stream** (§11 #227;
`FFmpegRewrap+Plan.swift`). **Copies:** the first video stream is `vp9`,
ffprobe's `profile` is 0 or 2, its `r_frame_rate` is `N/D` with both
positive integers, ffmpeg's snapped copy exits 0, the copy's video packets
sit at `p0 + k·D/N` s for every k with `p0` a whole number of frames (the
frame check), and the file then probes and decodes its first frame.
**Re-encodes, as before:** VP9 Profile 1, 3 or unknown; no usable
`r_frame_rate`; every variable-rate source — dropped frames, jittered times,
a header rate that is not the true rate — because its copy fails the frame
check; a user's ffmpeg whose `setts` has no `prescale` (the copy fails); a
copy that will not decode; every non-VP9 codec, untouched. The copy is the
stream copy's command with `-bsf:v setts=time_base=D/N:prescale=1` after
`-c:v copy` and `-f mp4`: an MP4-format file under the unchanged
`<stem>-<16 hex>.mov` digest name, its sound copied when AAC or ALAC and
AAC at 256k otherwise. Before the partial is moved into place the product
reads the copy's own packet times (`ffprobe … -show_entries
stream=time_base:packet=pts`, `FFmpegRewrap.keepsFrameGrid`) and keeps it
only when they are consecutive frames; a copy that fails that, fails in
ffmpeg or will not open is discarded and the source re-encoded once, under
the same policy (under `ask`, with a second question). **Measured at spec
time** (ffmpeg 9.0.2, 128×72 `testsrc2`, `AVAssetImageGenerator` at zero
tolerance asked for frame k at exactly k·D/N, "exact" meaning the answer's
time equals the request's): an unsnapped copy is exact for 20 of 60 requests
at 30 fps and 2 of 60 at 30000/1001, the re-encode 60 of 60 on both, and the
snapped copy 60 of 60 at 30 (Profile 0 and 2) and 30000/1001 and n of n at
24, 25, 60, 120, 24000/1001 and 19001/317, with packet times identical to
the re-encode's; a source with every fifth frame dropped and one with times
jittered by 0/9/18 ms both report 30/1 in both rate fields, fail the frame
check at k = 2 and re-encode; the frame check on a 10-minute, 18,000-frame,
101 MB copy took 0.11 s. Measured by `ffmpeg_rewrap_proof.py` T1–T3 and
V1–V8 (the proof README's B80 section).
