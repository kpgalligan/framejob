# 7.7 Import

`MediaImporter.probe(url)` (async): loads `duration`, video/audio tracks,
`naturalSize` × `preferredTransform` (so rotated phone footage reports its
display size), `nominalFrameRate` (converted to the nearest rational
among the common rates, else `[round(fps × 1000), 1000]`), alpha
(`hasAlphaChannel` on the format description), and returns the `media[]`
descriptor for `add_media` — and for `relink_media`'s `probe`, so a relink
takes the new file's duration, size, rate and flags (§11, #63). A video
track must also decode: the probe reads its first frame to 32BGRA through an
`AVAssetReader` (`DecoderSupport.decodesFirstFrame`) and refuses the file as
`undecodable`, in a sentence naming the codec, only when the reader fails
with "no decoder" (-11833) or "undecodable media data" (-11855); damaged data
in a codec the Mac decodes imports as before, and the refusal is never
offered to ffmpeg (§11, #216). Every process opts in to macOS's supplemental
VP9 and AV1 decoders first (`DecoderSupport.enable()`: at launch, before a
cache pass, before a composition looks up a track).
Thumbnails: 160 px tall, one per second up to 600, JPEG in
`cache/thumbs`. Peaks: mono float, 100 buckets/s, in
`cache/peaks`. Both run in the background after import and the timeline
redraws as they land — and both are SKIPPED when the cache's completion
marker matches the file (§5.6); a relinked, changed, partial or unmarked
cache is rebuilt. Image files (PNG/JPEG/HEIC) are probed with ImageIO
and become `kind: image` media.

Formats measured on 2026-10-02 on macOS 26.5.1 (25F80), Apple M2 Max
(`hw_av1` false, `hw_vp9` true after the opt-in, `hw_hevc` true), by
`tools/proof/formats_probe.py` — the product's own `MediaImporter.waitForProbe`
(no ffmpeg rewrap) and one decode of the first frame, sound buffer or image,
over 1 s, 128×72 ffmpeg-made files. **Opens** (probe and decode): MOV, MP4,
M4V, MPEG-TS and AVI containers; H.264, HEVC (`hvc1`), ProRes 422, MJPEG and
MPEG-4 Part 2 video; VP9 Profile 0 and Profile 2 (10-bit) in MP4; M4A (AAC
and ALAC), ADTS AAC, MP3, WAV, AIFF, CAF, FLAC, Ogg Opus and Ogg Vorbis audio.
**Images** (ImageIO): PNG, JPEG, TIFF (RGB), GIF, BMP and HEIC open.
**Refused as undecodable** (§11, #216): AV1 in MP4 ("mp4_av1.mp4 is AV1
video, which needs an M3 or later Mac.", `isPlayable` false) and VP9 Profile
1 (4:4:4) in MP4 ("mp4_vp9_p1.mp4 is VP9 Profile 1 video, which this Mac
cannot decode; VP9 Profiles 0 and 2 import.", though `isPlayable` is true).
**Refused** (the probe throws `unreadable`): MKV (H.264, AV1) and WebM (VP9
with Opus, VP8 with Vorbis) (§11). **Probe-only**: a 640×360 H.264 MP4 whose
first GOP was overwritten with 2048 random bytes imports, and its first frame
does not decode ("Cannot Decode", -11821). VP8 in MP4 was not made (ffmpeg
wrote no packets). VP9 and AV1 in MOV were not measured: ffmpeg will not
write either into `.mov`. AV1 decode depends on hardware, and a host with an
AV1 hardware decoder (M3 or later) may answer differently. WebP was not
measured (no encoder on the host).

**WebM and Matroska open by a built-in rewrap** (§11, #216-R; issue batch
B73). When AVFoundation refuses a file whose first four bytes are an EBML
header (1A 45 DF A3), the import funnel (`MediaImporter.probeAllowingRewrap`)
hands it to `ContainerRewrap.assist` with no question: the core indexes the
container (`ReelContainer` over `rc_container_*`) and `AVAssetWriter` copies
its first video and first audio stream, unchanged, into
`~/Library/Application Support/Reelcut/Rewrapped/<stem>-<16 hex>.mov` — the
folder and the digest name ffmpeg's conversions use, so a second import
reuses the copy — and the copy is probed as any movie is. **Copied:** VP9
(with a `vpcC` built from the first keyframe), AV1, H.264 and HEVC video;
Opus (OpusHead as the cookie, each packet's length from its TOC) and AAC
(an ES_Descriptor cookie) sound. **Before anything is written:** VP8, AV1
without hardware decode and VP9 Profile 1/3 are refused in #216's sentences;
a codec the copy does not carry (Vorbis, FLAC, MP3, AC-3, MPEG-2 and the
rest, or a compressed or encrypted track) is refused as "`<file>` has
Vorbis audio, which Reelcut cannot copy out of a WebM or Matroska file." and
alone falls through to the opt-in ffmpeg step; the core's `required_bytes`
(the copied payload, 64 bytes a sample and 16 MiB) is compared with the
destination volume's free space for important usage, and a copy that will
not fit is refused as "Not enough free space to open `<file>`: its video and
sound need about `<size>` on “`<volume>`”, which has `<size>` free. Nothing
was copied." **Timing:** video lying within a quarter frame of its rate's
grid is written on it (frame k at k·den in a timescale of num), so an
exact-time request lands on the file's own frame; a variable-rate file keeps
its millisecond times. **A failure** leaves no file (the copy is written as
`<stem>.partial.mov` and moved into place) and says "Could not copy
`<file>` into a movie: `<reason>`." A copy #216's one-frame decode still
refuses is deleted and refused in the source's name. Dropped: every track
after the first of each kind, subtitles, chapters, attachments and VP9
alpha. Measured by `tools/proof/container_rewrap_probe.py` and
`tools/proof/container_import_proof.py` (the proof README's B73 section).
