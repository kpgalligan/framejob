# 4.3d Inspector — the Clip tab's sections: audio, text and shape

Continues §4.3b, the video sections. The tab strip and the Clip tab are §4.3a; the Media, Document and Assistant tabs are §4.3c.

**Audio sections** — clips whose audio is enabled and whose media has an
audio track, or nest clips with audio properties (`InspectorTab.hasAudio`).

- Volume % (the field 0–3200, the slider 0–400 with the knob pinned at its
  end above that; dB shown; §11 AU-1); **Loudness**, a read-out of the clip's
  integrated loudness and true peak over the window that plays at volume 1,
  from the loudness cache (`−41.7 LUFS · −18.5 dBTP`; "measuring…" while the
  cache is pending, "—" for several clips or none measurable), and **At
  volume**, the same plus 20·log10 of the base volume (§11 AU-11, AU-12,
  AU-15: held through a live drag, the Loudness row updating at its end),
  read through `AudioLimiterModel` with the peak at most the ceiling while
  the clip's limiter is on (§11 AU-26) — on a clip with a stage the cache
  leaves out (an active EQ, hum, isolation, noise reduction, a gate, a
  de-esser, a compressor or a voice), the last R0 a Normalize or Enhance
  Voice measured through its chain for the clip's current plan and chain,
  else the cache figure prefixed "≈ " with the tooltip "≈ before the clip's
  processing; Normalize or Enhance Voice measures through it." (§11 AU-64 (g));
  Mute; the **Normalize** row's Mode popup (Peak | Loudness) and Target field
  (LUFS, −50…−5, quick −14/−16/−23, live in Loudness only), app-wide in
  UserDefaults (`FrameJobNormalizeMode`, `FrameJobNormalizeTargetLUFS`;
  default Loudness and −16, §11 AU-25), which the button and Clip ▸ Normalize
  follow (§11 AU-15); and **Normalize** — in Peak mode one press sets the
  clip's volume so its own loudest moment lands at −1 dBFS, measured from
  the waveform cache over the source window that actually plays; in
  Loudness mode so its integrated loudness lands at the target under
  −1 dBTP and ×32, turning the clip's limiter on (its own kept, the ceiling
  at most −1) when the peak needs it and solving the volume through the
  limiter's model (§11 AU-24), every clip to one target; a clip with such
  a stage is rendered through its chain and solved on what comes out, the
  limiter verified by render, under the Measuring Sound sheet (a bar, "Clip
  n of N…", Cancel), and committed only if the project and scope are still
  the ones it read (§11 AU-64, AU-65). It
  REPLACES the volume rather than multiplying it (so pressing twice changes
  nothing), writes the clip's BASE volume even when the playhead is inside
  an action, leaves `muted` alone, and gives each clip of a selection its
  own gain (in Peak mode not a match; in Loudness mode the match). A clip quieter than about −31 dBFS cannot reach −1 through the
  model's 32× ceiling: it is normalised as loud as it goes and the result
  says where it landed. Also Clip ▸ Normalize. With an action selected it
  carries a boxed mono "clip" tag: it writes the clip whatever is selected.
  Under it, a row with **Enhance Voice** (road map "audio" AP6, §11
  AU-60…AU-65): each clip measured pre-chain (its loudness, its speech and
  pause steps, the pauses' floor and peak, the sibilant share) and set up for
  speech with ordinary settings — Rumble Cut, isolation 0.8 or noise
  reduction 0.5 and a gate when the pauses are noisy, the leveller when the
  speech's short-term loudness range is over 6 LU (§11 AU-67), the de-esser at a 5 %
  sibilant share, a compressor at the speech's median peak with its makeup,
  the limiter at most −1 dBTP and the volume to the Normalize row's Target
  measured through all of them — as one undo step, under the Measuring Sound
  sheet; enabled where Normalize is. Also Clip ▸ Enhance Voice.
- Fade in / fade out in seconds (also the corner handles on the clip).
  The rows store the length typed, off the frame grid (§11 #355). In shape
  and Out shape (format version 29, §11 AU-114): Linear (the default),
  S-Curve, +3 dB (fast start) or −3 dB (slow start); base-only, as the duck
  is (§11 CI-3, AU-88).
- Ducking: "lower other audio to N % while this clip plays", with
  attack / release, each stored as typed, off the frame grid (§11 #355).
  Duck by (format version 29, §11 AU-110..AU-113): Clip Length — the
  whole clip extent, the default — or Speech: only while the clip's speech
  plays, from the spans saved on its media, widened by Pre-roll and Hold
  (0–5 s, 0.5 s each by default, enabled in Speech). Choosing Speech is ONE
  edit with a `set_media_speech` for each media without spans, from its
  voice cache; the popup is disabled while the duck follows the clip's
  length for a selection holding a clip that is not a media clip with
  sound or whose speech has not been detected yet. A speech ducker draws
  its own multiplier as a 1 pt yellow line across its audio region on the
  timeline. Realised as volume ramps folded into the other clips'
  envelopes by the core.
- Mix to mono — a fold, not a gain, so it is sample work like the three
  below and lives with them at the head of the tap's Mix stage (§7.2). It
  averages EVERY channel of the composition track and writes the average to
  all of them, and it is inert below two channels. Detach Audio (⌥⌘D; also
  Clip menu).
- Volume actions share the action lane.
- M3 — Pan, "Smooth volume levels" (compressor), "Remove noise" (the
  Noise Reduction section). Unlike everything above, these three — and Mix to mono, which
  joined them in road map 2's phase 5 — cannot be a gain curve, so
  they are not folded into the core's envelope: they are sample work, done
  in Swift by one `MTAudioProcessingTap` per composition audio track, and
  the SAME tap serves the preview and the export (the mix reaches both
  `AVPlayerItem.audioMix` and `AVAssetReaderAudioMixOutput.audioMix`, and
  taps were measured to run on each). A track no clip of which uses them
  gets no tap and is bit-identical to before they existed — on an EXPORT,
  which is where that promise is worth something. A preview build made for
  the level meter (§4.4) taps every audio track, inert ones included,
  because a meter that lights only on the tracks carrying a compressor is
  worse than none; there is no file there to be identical to.
  - Pan is a balance: one gain per channel, constant power across the
    sweep and unity at the centre, so 0 is exactly a bypass and a hard
    pan is +3 dB in the channel it goes to. Inaudible on a clip whose
    source is mono — the widening to stereo happens after the tap. **On a
    clip that also asks for Mix to mono it is a TRUE pan**, because the
    fold runs first and both channels then carry the same signal; that
    ordering is forced rather than chosen, since folding AFTER a pan is
    algebraically a gain change — `(g_L·x + g_R·x)/2 = ((g_L+g_R)/2)·x` —
    and would make the pan control read as broken.
  - The compressor is a channel-strip insert on the track, ahead of the
    fader: its envelope carries across a cut and the volume, fades and
    ducking apply after it. Its attack and release are ticks in the
    schema but sub-frame, and the pane shows them in ms.
  - Noise reduction is a short-time spectral attenuator over a noise
    floor tracked from the clip itself — not a gate, and it delays the
    track it runs on by 512 samples (10.7 ms at 48 kHz).
- Voice Isolation (format version 31, §11 AU-50…AU-57), before Noise
  Reduction: an "Isolate voice" check (on writes `IsolationCache.defaultAmount`,
  0.8; off writes 0), an Amount row 0–100 % (quick picks 50, 80, 100) and a
  Status read-out for a single clip — "Rendering…", "Ready", "Plays without
  it at this speed" (a time-scaled, ramped or freeze segment, AU-55), "Waits
  for the reversed copy", or "Not rendered: <why>"; "—" for several —
  refreshed on `.reversedMediaDidChange`, which the document posts when a
  render lands and when one fails. A note row says it "also reduces room
  echo": de-reverb is this control (AU-56). With an action selected it
  writes the action's end and the Amount animates, from 0 when the base has
  none; the render is made when the base or any end asks (AU-88). The
  render is Apple's `AUSoundIsolation` offline per clip window (§7.2); the
  tap crossfades it with the dry sound by Amount BEFORE every stage above
  (AU-53, AU-54), per frame inside an action (AU-87).
- Voice Effect (format version 34, §11 AU-140…AU-148), right after Voice
  Isolation: an Effect popup — "None", then the sixteen kinds in E8's order
  titled with their group, "Voice: Pitch", "Voice: High", "Voice: Deep",
  "Voice: Robot", "Tone: Bass" … "Space: Cathedral" — where a kind writes
  `{kind, amount: current or 1, pitch: null}` (every kind starts at its own
  pitch) and None writes null; an Amount row 0–100 % (quick 25, 50, 100); a
  Pitch row −12…12 st at one decimal (quick −12, −5, 0, 5, 12), enabled only
  for Pitch, High and Deep and showing the stored pitch or the kind's own (0,
  +5, −5); and a Status read-out in Voice Isolation's words. Summary "off" or
  the kind's title and amount. Base-only (AU-140). The render is a fixed chain
  of Apple's units offline per clip window (§7.2), played in place of the
  clip's sound after its isolation and before every stage above (AU-143).
  There is no Clip menu item for it yet.
- Limiter (§11 AU-20..AU-23, AU-26): a section after Mix, the chain's last
  stage. "Limit peaks" (on writes `{ceiling_db: -1, release: 60000}`),
  Ceiling (dBTP, −24…0, one decimal, quick −1/−2/−3/−6) and Release (ms,
  1…1000, quick 50/100/200/500, off the frame grid); summary "−1.0 dBTP" or
  "off"; Reset clears it. With an action selected it writes the action's
  end, "Limit peaks" turned off there keying it off (§11 AU-88). A lookahead
  true-peak limiter in the same tap, told the clip's base volume because the
  fader is after it; it delays its whole track by 158 samples, led out like
  noise reduction's (§7.2).
- Channels, EQ and Hum (§11 AU-30..AU-38): three sections after Voice
  Isolation and before Noise Reduction, the head of the clip's live chain in
  its order. Channels
  is one popup — Both, Left Only, Right Only, Swap Left and Right — its
  summary its word. EQ draws the response first (the stage's own design at
  48 kHz, 256 log-spaced points 20 Hz–20 kHz, the EQ and the hum together,
  dimmed when bypassed, none for a mixed selection), then Bypass, the presets
  Rumble Cut, De-mud and Presence (each writes only its own slot and turns
  Bypass off, so they combine), then per slot — High-pass, Low shelf, Band 1,
  Band 2, Band 3, High shelf, Low-pass — a checkbox writing the slot's default
  (the bands at 300, 1000 and 4000 Hz), Frequency (Hz, a field with quick
  picks), Gain (dB −24…24, quick −6/−3/0/3/6), Q (0.1…10, quick
  0.5/0.7/1/2/4) and Slope (12 or 24 dB/oct) as the slot has them; summary
  "off", "bypassed" or "N on"; Reset clears it. Hum: "Remove hum" (on writes
  the defaults), Mains (50 or 60 Hz), Depth (dB 6…60, quick 12/24/30/48) and
  Harmonics (1…8, quick 1/2/4/8); summary "60 Hz · 4" or "off". With an
  action selected all three write the action's end, an EQ slot turned off
  there keyed off as `audio.eq.<slot>` (§11 AU-88); a slope, the mains and
  the harmonics switch at the end. The pick counts in the Loudness readout (pan · fold · pick, §11
  AU-35); the EQ and hum do not.
- Gate and De-esser (§11 AU-40..AU-48): two sections after Noise Reduction
  and before Compressor, the chain's order. Both thresholds are dB RELATIVE
  to the clip's measured loudness (the Volume section's Loudness figure,
  AU-41). Gate: "Gate pauses" (on writes the defaults), Threshold (dB re
  loudness −40…0, quick −20/−15/−10/−6), Ratio (1…20, quick 2/4/10/20),
  Range (dB 1…80, quick 10/20/40/80), Attack (ms 0…100, quick 1/5/10), Hold
  (ms 0…500, quick 0/50/100/200), Release (ms 1…2000, quick 50/100/250/500);
  summary "−10 dB" or "off". De-esser: "Reduce sibilance" (on writes the
  defaults), Frequency (Hz 2000…12 000, quick 4000/5000/6000/8000), Threshold
  (dB re loudness −30…+20, quick −6/−3/0/3), Range (dB 1…24, quick 3/6/10);
  summary "6.0 kHz" or "off". Each ends with a Status read-out ("Opens above
  −31.8 dBFS" / "Acts above …", "Waits for its loudness", "Not gating: too
  short", "… too quiet", "… no sound") and a Loudness read-out ("−21.8
  LUFS", "measuring…"). Reset clears each; with an action selected both
  write the action's end (§11 AU-88), and a level is measured for a clip
  whose end alone asks for one. Until the
  clip's loudness is measured, neither stage acts (AU-42).
- Leveller (§11 AU-66): between Gate and De-esser, closed by default.
  "Ride the level" (on writes the defaults), Target (dB re loudness −20…10,
  quick −6/−3/0/3), Max Boost (dB 0…24, quick 6/12/18), Max Cut (dB 0…24,
  quick 3/6/12), Response (s 0.5…10, one decimal, quick 1/2/4); a Status
  read-out ("Aims at −41.7 LUFS", "Waits for its loudness", "Waits for its
  speech", "Not levelling: …") and Loudness; summary the target ("0 dB") or
  "off". Each control writes the whole group; with an action selected it
  writes the action's end, where Response takes effect at the end. Until the
  clip's loudness and speech are measured it does not act (AU-66 (e)).

**Text sections**

- Content (multi-line), font family / style / size, color, alignment
  (left / center / right and top / middle / bottom within the box), line
  spacing, letter spacing, and an UPPERCASE checkbox in the Style section
  that writes `text.case` (format version 9) — a rendering transform applied
  before layout, so it changes the measured box but never the word count.
- On a word-caption page (a text clip with `word_style`, road map 8), the
  Content field keeps the page's `words` while an edit keeps the word count
  and sends `words: []` in the same `set_text` when it changes it — decided
  PER CLIP, and never `words` inside an action's end patch (§11 W-2, W-6).
  A styled page left untimed that way carries the Content section's
  footnote "Not timed — regenerate to time it again", and the Animation
  section's three rows are greyed with "Word style wins; the reveal does
  not apply." (§11 W-5). Nothing in the inspector writes
  `word_style`: restyling is regenerating (§11 W-10).
- Outline (width, color); Backdrop (fill, opacity, padding, corner
  radius); Shadow (shared section).
- Box: auto-size, or fixed width with wrapping.
- Animation on position / opacity / scale is Actions, like every other
  property. The built-in REVEALS are their own thing and are
  `text.animation` (format version 5): a *Reveal* section (titled
  *Animation* until road map 9's phase 2; the id is unchanged) of three
  controls — a kind popup (None, Typewriter, Word Fade, Line Rise), In and
  Out — written through `set_text` as one whole object, which is the SECTION
  saying all three controls and not a rule about patches: a patch of a nested
  object merges key by key (§5.2, §6), so an agent may send `animation` with
  one key in it and keep the other two. In and Out are DURATIONS measured
  from the clip's own first and last frame, not times.
  The section cannot show the reveal's PROGRESS, and deliberately does not
  try: how far along a reveal is, is a fact about the frame under the
  playhead rather than about the clip, and the core computes it per frame
  into the recipe (§7.3) so the preview, `render_frame` and an export
  cannot disagree about it.
- Presets: Title, Subtitle, Lower third, Caption — set face, size,
  backdrop and position in one click. Sizes are fractions of the canvas's
  short side, positions of its width and height (§11 VR-41).

**Shape sections**

- Kind: rectangle, ellipse, line, arrow (a rounded rectangle is a
  rectangle with a corner radius). Stroke width
  and color, fill color and opacity, arrowheads at start / end, corner
  radius, Shadow.
- Format version 6: *Gradient* — a Kind popup (Linear, Radial), an Angle
  row disabled for Radial, the stop list as an at-and-colour row apiece
  with Add and Remove, and a Reset that sends `{"gradient": null}` and so
  brings the flat fill back. Every row is DISABLED for `line` and `arrow`,
  which have no area to fill and keep their stroke colour — the field is
  still stored and range-checked on them (§5.2), exactly as `radius` is on
  every kind. Every key of `gradient` is re-sent on every write, which is the
  SECTION knowing all of them rather than a rule about patches: a patch of a
  nested object merges key by key (§5.2, §6), so an agent may send `gradient`
  with one key in it and keep the rest — and a `stops` LIST it sends replaces
  the whole list, which is why the row's Add and Remove re-send every stop.
- M3: *Spotlight* — which darkens the canvas outside a rectangle or
  ellipse, with an optional 1–2× zoom of the region (1 is none) — is a
  `video.spotlight` prop and appears in the **Picture** sub-tab, not here.
