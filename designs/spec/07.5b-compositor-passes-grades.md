# 7.5b Compositor — the passes, mattes, transitions and grades

The `Compositor` class, its pixel formats, step 0, camera log, the scopes' tap, the HLG working space, the output pass and linear light are §7.5a.

- **One pass per layer, and TEN pipelines from eight fragment
  functions.** The vertex stage places a quad from a per-layer uniform
  block (canvas size, center, source size, scale, rotation, crop window as
  UV bounds); the fragment stage samples with the crop window and runs the
  order of operations written out at the head of `Compositor.metal` —
  the corner-radius mask (a rounded-rect SDF in source pixels),
  brightness/contrast/saturation/hue, chroma key (M3), the filters, the hidden
  regions (format version 4), tone (step 4c, below), the wheels (step 4c.1,
  below), the curves and hue curves (step 4c.2, below) and the look (step
  4d, below), the
  spotlight, coverage (corner radius
  × nest clip rectangle × matte), the transition weight and opacity — and
  outputs premultiplied colour. Text and shape textures are already
  premultiplied and take the same path. A video
  frame is premultiplied only when its buffer says so — its
  `kCVImageBufferAlphaChannelModeKey` attachment is `PremultipliedAlpha` —
  and straight otherwise, an unlabelled frame included (§11 G-35 (b)). A text
  layer's opacity is the recipe's, times `1 − word_dim × pause_dim` on a
  timed page with no backdrop (§11 W-16; `RecipeLayer.drawnOpacity`).
  Missing media draws a slate.
  Two independent specializations of that stage give four layer states,
  the shadow's quad is the fifth, the matte resolve the sixth, the dissolve mix the seventh, the adjustment pass the eighth (below), step 0's input pass the ninth (§7.5a) and the blurred qualifier's selection map the tenth (below). **`kGatherFilters`** is a function
  constant: a layer that needs a neighbourhood filter (blur, sharpen) or a
  blurred hidden region compiles the gather loops in, and every other
  layer has them compiled OUT rather than branched around.
  **`framejob_layer_blend_fragment`** is the second entry point, added at
  format version 6: it reads the destination through `float4 dst
  [[color(0)]]` — programmable blending, which needs no ping-pong target
  on Apple Silicon — and computes `video.blend`'s combination itself,
  where the plain entry point returns its colour and lets the
  fixed-function stage apply source-over. Both share one `static inline`
  helper carrying the whole stage, because Metal cannot call one
  `fragment` function from another; a `normal` layer's pixels therefore do
  not depend on which of the two drew it, since the general formula
  reduces algebraically to the fixed-function factors.
  **The two blend pipelines are built with fixed-function blending
  DISABLED, and that is a correctness requirement nothing in the toolchain
  enforces.** Metal builds a programmable-blend pipeline with blending
  still on — no error, no validation warning — and the double blend is
  invisible over an OPAQUE destination, because the fragment stage returns
  the finished composite, whose alpha is `as + ab·(1 - as)` = 1 over any
  opaque backdrop, and `oneMinusSourceAlpha` is then 0 whatever the layer's
  own opacity is. **What decides it is the DESTINATION's alpha, not the
  layer's**, and phase 6 got that backwards first: the partial-opacity
  check written for the job passed against a build with the guard deleted,
  as did every other fixture in the script, all of which put an opaque card
  underneath. It takes a destination alpha below one AND a source that does
  not cover it — `tools/proof/phase6_proof.py`'s section 6b, the one check
  in that script that reads the ALPHA channel, where the mutant lands 32/255
  high while the colour moves by two.
- **The adjustment pass** (§11 GR-6): an adjustment clip's
  layer (`source.kind` `adjustment`) has no texture. `encodeLayers` hands it
  to `drawAdjustment` (`Compositor+Adjustment.swift`) instead of `prepare`,
  which draws the shadow's full-frame quad with the eighth pipeline,
  `framejob_adjust_fragment`, built with fixed-function blending OFF for the
  blend pipelines' reason. It reads what is already drawn in the target it
  is drawing into (`[[color(0)]]` — the canvas pass, a matte group's or a
  dissolve half's target), un-premultiplies it, applies the layer's colour
  adjust (`gradeSample`, the chroma key off), tone (`gradeTone`, behind
  `toneEnabled`), its wheels (`gradeWheels`, behind `wheelsEnabled`, §11
  GR-11), its curves (`gradeCurves`, behind `curvesEnabled`, the strip at
  `texture(3)`, GR-12), its hue curves (`gradeHueCurves`, behind
  `hueCurvesEnabled`, GR-32), its look (`gradeLook`, behind `lookEnabled`, the lattice at
  `texture(2)`) and point filters (the canvas's uv as their local
  position), premultiplies by the destination's own alpha — so alpha is
  preserved — and returns `mix(dst, graded, w)`, `w` the layer's opacity
  times the matte coverage sampled as step 7c samples it. So it grades
  everything behind it in its container, the background included, and
  nothing in front; under a matte it grades only inside it. An adjustment
  layer whose colour and wheels are neutral and six curves the identity, that has
  no look and no point filter above 0 draws nothing (`isNeutralAdjustment`), so
  Show Ungraded's
  frame is the frame without it. The pass reads the same `LayerUniforms` as
  a layer (128 floats since GR-11).
- **The qualifier** (grading-2 G2-4, §11 GR-13) is the adjustment pass's
  alone. `drawAdjustment` binds a second block at fragment `buffer(1)`,
  `QualifierUniforms` — sixteen scalar floats, 64 bytes, the same layout
  contract as `LayerUniforms` (`QualifierProps.swift` mirrors the shader
  field for field): the hue, saturation and luma groups of four (enabled,
  then the three numbers), `invert`, `selection`, `enabled` and `blurred`.
  With `enabled` 1, `qualifierWeight` reads the destination colour the pass
  un-premultiplied, BEFORE step 3 — so the grade never moves what it
  selects: HSV hue against the band (distance wrapping at 360) times the
  chroma guard `smoothstep(0.02, 0.06, C)`; HSV saturation C / max times the
  near-black guard `smoothstep(0.03, 0.10, max)`; Rec. 709 Y′; each range 1
  inside and falling to 0 across its softness OUTSIDE each edge by
  `smoothstep` (a hard step at softness 0), saturation and Y′ saturated first;
  the product, inverted by `invert`. The weight becomes `w = saturate(opacity
  × coverage × q)`; with `enabled` 0 it is `opacity × coverage × 1`, the same
  float, so an unqualified pass renders byte-identical. A layer the recipe
  marks `selection` returns `float4(float3(q × coverage), 1)` — opaque grey,
  white fully selected, the strength not applied — and draws even when its
  grade is neutral or its opacity 0 (`uniforms(for:…, ignoringOpacity:)`).
  `LayerUniforms` does not grow. **The cleanup blur** (format 21, §11 GR-35):
  when a range is on, `blur` is above 0, σ = 24·blur canvas pixels ÷
  `canvasPerDestX` is at least 0.5 destination pixels (capped at 100,
  `ColorQualifier.selectionSigma`) and MPS runs on the device, `drawAdjustment`
  first suspends the destination pass, renders q of every pixel of the target
  into an `r16Float` map of the destination's size (the tenth pipeline,
  `framejob_qualifier_map_fragment`, blending off), blurs it into its pair with
  `MPSImageGaussianBlur` (`.clamp`, the shadow's kernel cache), and the
  adjustment draw resumes the pass with `.load`, binds the blurred map at
  `texture(0)` and sets `blurred`, so the fragment reads q there instead of
  computing it (`Compositor+Selection.swift`). Only q is blurred: the coverage
  is multiplied after, unblurred, so Show Selection draws blurred q ×
  coverage. Every other adjustment pass binds the placeholder at `texture(0)`
  with `blurred` 0 and renders byte-identical. The pair (2 × 16.6 MB at 4K) is
  held per renderer once drawn, replaced when the destination's size
  changes.
- **This compositor blends in the ENCODED sRGB space, deliberately.**
  Nothing in the pipeline uses an `_srgb` pixel format — the attachment is
  `.rgba16Float` holding encoded values (§11 PR-1) — so every stage works on
  encoded values, and so does the blend. "Multiply of 50 % grey over 50 % grey is 25 %" is a statement
  about ENCODED values and is FALSE in linear light, where the answer is
  about **61**/255 — three steps below the 64 the encoded multiply gives
  (60.95 against 64.25 unrounded), and three times the ±1/255 the proof's
  blend checks allow themselves, so the two readings cannot both pass. The
  number is part of the sentence: a reader who checks it and finds it wrong
  stops believing the paragraph it is in. A reader who knows colour will
  want to wrap the blend in a linearise/encode pair: that would change
  every blended pixel, break the
  measured 64 / 192 / 255 the proof asserts, and make the compositor
  disagree with itself, since the grade, the filters and the transitions
  have all worked in encoded space since M1. If linear light is ever
  wanted it is a decision for the WHOLE pipeline, not for one stage. The
  same sentence stands in `Compositor.metal`'s order-of-operations comment
  and in `tools/proof/phase6_proof.py`'s docstring, because it is the one
  thing about this compositor a competent reader will try to "fix".
- **Transitions** are one pass over the pair: dissolve = both with
  progress-weighted opacity; fade black/white = out fades to the color
  then in fades from it; push/slide = offset by progress; wipe = a UV
  threshold; zoom = scale ramp. The FOUR wipe directions (format version 7)
  are one shader branch and four vectors, as the four pushes are: the
  branch takes `local.y` when `|dirY| > |dirX|` and flips on a negative
  component, and the edge is measured in each layer's OWN box — so a
  different-size pair does not meet at one edge, which is the price of
  every nested wipe working and the common same-size pair being exact. The
  `progress` the pass reads is already eased (§7.3); nothing here knows
  about `curve`. A ROUTED dissolve or zoom (§7.3 `dissolve`, §11 MT-8) is
  not such a pair: `Compositor+Dissolve.swift` draws each half, unweighted,
  into a destination-sized pool target cleared transparent, then lays the
  two on the pass with the seventh pipeline (`framejob_dissolve_fragment`
  over the shadow's full-frame quad) as (1 − p)·out + p·in, a routed zoom
  scaling each half there about its anchor. A layer mid-transition always draws
  `normal`, and the core is what says so (§7.3), so this shader has no
  special case for it and none may be added. Shadows (M3) render the
  layer's alpha to a canvas-sized offscreen mask (one shared pair), blur with
  `MPSImageGaussianBlur`, draw first — and the MASK pass takes the layer's
  gather specialization WITHOUT its blend, because the mask asks about the
  layer's SHAPE, which no blend mode changes. That separation is DEFENSIVE
  and not load-bearing, and phase 6's mutation run is what settled it: the
  natural fear — a `multiply` evaluated over a destination cleared to
  transparent black is 0 everywhere, so a blended layer's shadow silently
  vanishes while the layer itself looks right — is true of a naive
  `src · dst` and FALSE of the premultiplied composite this shader runs,
  whose `(1 - ab)·src` term carries the source through an empty destination:
  at `ab` = 0 the formula reduces to `src` exactly, for all eight modes. A
  build that passes the blend variant here renders every shadow correctly.
  Keep the separation — it costs nothing, it says what the mask pass means,
  and a future formula without that term would make the hazard real — but
  do not restate it as a bug it is holding back. The shadow's own draw stays
  fixed-function source-over whatever the layer's mode (a shadow is light
  falling on the backdrop, not the layer's ink), while the REFLECTION
  inherits the blend, since it is part of the layer.
- **Mattes** (format version 14, §11 MT-3, MT-6; `Compositor+Matte.swift`).
  Before the destination pass opens, each group the recipe's `mattes`
  lists (deepest container first) is drawn by the ordinary layer
  pipelines — the layers whose `into` names it, each the way the canvas
  draws a layer, shadow and reflection included, though the core sends a
  matte layer none — into a destination-sized `rgba16Float` target cleared
  transparent, so a matte never suspends the destination pass as a shadow
  does. Each distinct `cut` set is then resolved into one destination-sized
  `r8Unorm` coverage cleared to 1: the resolve pipeline (the shadow's
  full-frame quad and `framejob_matte_resolve_fragment`, source factor
  zero, destination factor `sourceColor`) multiplies in each matte's value
  at the pixel — its alpha, 1 − alpha, the Rec. 709 luma of its
  premultiplied colour, or 1 − that luma (`kMatteNone`…`kMatteLumaInverted`
  = `TrackMatte`'s case index, append only). Both layer entry points take
  that coverage at **`texture(1)`** and step 7 multiplies the fragment's
  coverage by it, sampled at the fragment's CANVAS-normalised position, so
  Preview Quality, a shadow's canvas-sized silhouette pass (which binds it
  too, so the shadow follows the cut shape) and a reflection, cut where it
  lands, all read one coverage. An unmatted layer binds the 1×1 white
  placeholder there (MT0's route V1). A layer with a non-null `into` is
  never drawn on the canvas. The targets are pooled per destination size:
  a matte's composite goes back to the pool once every cut set naming it
  is resolved, and the pool keeps what the renderer's last 30 frames held
  (`MattePlan`, `MattePool`; §11 MT-9). The outgoing half
  of a dissolve weighs its partner's cover by its own coverage, which is
  the partner's on one matted track (MT-3): the pair composites to
  m·mix(out, in, p) + (1 − m)·bg under a soft matte as under a hard one —
  reached only for a timeline pair of clips that are not nest clips, since
  every other dissolve is routed (§11 MT-8).
- **Tone (§11 GR-2, GR-3).** Step **4c**, after the
  hidden regions' own re-sample (4b) and before the point filters (5), runs
  `gradeTone` once per pixel on straight colour: exposure and the
  white-balance gains on LOCAL linear light — decoded (IEC 61966-2-1),
  multiplied, re-encoded inside the step, a colour transform like Color's
  hue turn, not the whole-pipeline linear light this section and PR-5 defer
  — then highlights, shadows, whites, blacks, vibrance and fade on encoded
  values. The per-layer block carries the nine as a tone group after
  `canvasPerDestY` — the nine, `toneEnabled`, `lookAmount` and `lookEnabled`
  (the look's two, below, which took the group's reserved tail), so 116
  floats, 464 bytes then, before the wheels below — and `toneEnabled` (1
  exactly when one of the nine is not 0) joins the effects gate's sum, so an
  untoned layer never enters 4c and renders byte-identical (GR0's route VF:
  0.000 ms at 1080p and 0.001 ms at 4K on an ungraded layer). Preview,
  `render_frame` and export share it through the one recipe.
- **Wheels (§11 GR-11).** Step **4c.1**, after tone (4c) and before the look
  (4d), runs `gradeWheels` once per pixel on encoded straight colour. Each
  wheel's per-channel offset is `master + k·hypot(x, y)·chroma(h)`, `chroma`
  the split tone's `lookHueChroma` (a fully saturated HSV hue less its own
  Rec. 709 luma) at h = atan2(y, x) in degrees taken into [0, 360) before the
  call (`wheelOffset`); then lift `v += o_lift·0.25·(1 − v)` (pivots at
  white), gain `v *= 1 + o_gain·0.5` (pivots at black), gamma
  `v = max(v, 0)^(2^(−o_gamma))` (holds both ends); k = 1. A push is
  luma-neutral on a grey pixel only. The block grows by a group of twelve
  after `lookEnabled` — `liftX`, `liftY`, `liftMaster`, `gammaX`, `gammaY`,
  `gammaMaster`, `gainX`, `gainY`, `gainMaster`, `wheelsEnabled`,
  `curvesEnabled` (read since G2-3, below) and one reserved (`hueCurvesEnabled`
  since T4-2, below) — so **128 floats, 512
  bytes** (32 × 16); `wheelsEnabled` (1 exactly when one of the nine is not
  0) joins the effects gate's sum, so an unwheeled layer never enters 4c.1
  and renders byte-identical. The adjustment pass runs 4c.1 in the same
  place. The look bake is unchanged: a look is Color, Light and split tone,
  and wheels are the clip's own.
- **Curves (§11 GR-12).** Step **4c.2**, after the wheels (4c.1) and before the
  look (4d), runs `gradeCurves` once per pixel on encoded straight colour:
  each channel through its own composite curve, `channel(master(v))`, read
  from the layer's curve **strip** at **`texture(3)`** — a 1,024 × 1
  `rgba32Float` texture (`rgba16Float` on a device without 32-bit float
  filtering) whose texel i holds red's, green's and blue's composite at
  i / 1023 in r, g and b — sampled linearly at `saturate(v)·(n−1)/n + 0.5/n`
  (4d's texel-centre rule, so the first and last texels are exactly 0 and 1).
  `CurveTable.swift` writes the texels from the points with the Fritsch–Carlson
  monotone spline (the editor draws the same function); `CurveTextures.swift`
  uploads and caches them by the curves in an LRU of eight held by
  `LookTextures`, beside the looks' cache. `curvesEnabled` — the wheels'
  group's eleventh float, reserved by GR-11, so the block stays **128 floats**
  — is 1 exactly when a curve is not the identity and joins the effects gate's
  sum, so an uncurved layer never enters 4c.2 and renders byte-identical. A
  2 × 1 identity placeholder is bound at `texture(3)` on every layer,
  shadow-mask and adjustment draw whose layer is uncurved (or whose strip
  could not be uploaded, which then draws uncurved with the flag at 0). The
  adjustment pass runs 4c.2 in the same place; the look bake is unchanged.
  A channel the recipe publishes as a blend `{from, to, t}` (§11 GR-34, two
  curves of different counts inside an action) is written into the strip as
  `(1 − t)·from(x) + t·to(x)`, each its own spline, in Double before the Float
  store; the step itself is unchanged, and each frame of the action is a new
  strip, as for any animated curve.
  **Amended by the hue curves (§11 GR-32, format 20).** The strip is **1,024
  × 2**: row 0 the composites above, sampled at y 0.25 (row 0's centre, exact
  in y, so every tone-curved render is unchanged); row 1 texel i holds Hue vs
  Hue's shift in turns (degrees / 360) in r and Hue vs Sat's gain in g at the
  hue 360·i / 1023, from the periodic Fritsch–Carlson spline (the secants
  wrapped across 360°). The placeholder is 2 × 2 (row 1 (0, 1, 0, 1)). After
  `gradeCurves`, in the layer stage and the adjustment pass, `gradeHueCurves`
  runs once per pixel behind **`hueCurvesEnabled`** (the group's twelfth
  float, formerly reserved; 1 exactly when a hue curve is not the identity,
  joining the effects gate's sum; `curvesEnabled` now means a TONE curve is
  not the identity): h, the HSV hue of the saturated colour (`hsvHue`, the
  helper `qualifierWeight` also calls), is read once and row 1 sampled there
  at y 0.75; the shift (when |turns| > 1e-6) is `gradeHueShift` — the
  colour's chroma along the fully saturated hue h + degrees less its luma
  (`lookHueChroma`), added to its Rec. 709 luma, scaled by the largest k ≤ 1
  keeping every channel inside [min(0, min rgb), max(1, max rgb)], so the HSV
  hue becomes exactly h + degrees with the luma kept and chroma given up only
  to stay in range; then the gain (when |g − 1| > 1e-6) mixes about the luma
  as Color's Saturation; the result is weighted by GR-13's chroma guard
  `smoothstep(0.02, 0.06, C)`, so a grey is never tinted. Step 3's Color Hue
  calls the same `gradeHueShift` (§11 GR-33): the hue read from the colour as
  step 3 leaves it, the turn wrapped into [0, 360), no chroma guard, gated on
  |hue| > 1e-4 rad.
- **Looks (§11 GR-7).** Step **4d**, right after 4c
  and before the point filters, runs `gradeLook` once per pixel: the layer's
  look lattice, a 3D texture bound at **`texture(2)`**, sampled trilinearly at
  `saturate(rgb)·(n−1)/n + 0.5/n` (the texel centres' span, so an identity
  lattice of any size returns its input) and mixed in by `lookAmount`. The
  lattice is BAKED, not drawn from a table: `LookTextures` runs the compute
  kernel `framejob_look_bake` once per `LookDefinition` the recipe publishes,
  taking the identity 33³ lattice through the stage's own `gradeSample`
  (chroma off), `gradeTone` and the split tone (`lookSplitTone`, its hues and
  amounts in the 16-byte `LookBakeUniforms`), each only when its block is not
  neutral, into an `rgba16Float` texture kept in a least-recently-used cache
  of eight; the bake waits for the GPU, so the first frame of a new
  definition pays it (under a millisecond, GR5a's record). `lookEnabled` (1
  exactly when the layer has a look with an amount above 0) joins the effects
  gate's sum, so a look-less layer never samples, and every draw through
  `framejob_layer_fragment`, `framejob_layer_blend_fragment` and
  `framejob_adjust_fragment` — a layer, its reflection, its shadow's mask
  pass, an adjustment clip — binds `texture(2)`: the look's lattice, or a 2³
  identity placeholder. Show Ungraded removes looks in the core (§7.3).
- **`.cube` LUTs (§11 GR-8).** A LUT look is drawn
  at the same step 4d by the same `gradeLook`, from a different lattice:
  `LutTextures` reads the media's `.cube` file (`MediaImporter.readLUT`,
  through the core's `rc_cube_parse`) and uploads its table as an
  `rgba16Float` `size³` 3D texture (red fastest, every value clamped to 0–1,
  alpha 1) in a least-recently-used cache of eight keyed by the file's path,
  byte size and modification date (so a file changed on disk is read again at
  the next frame), a file that cannot be read or is refused cached as a
  failure. The file is found through `RecipeSource.lookURLs(for:)` — the
  media URLs the document last published under the instruction's key
  (`Playback.update`), else the instruction's own map (an export's or
  `render_frame`'s fresh key) — never the instruction's alone, because adding,
  applying or relinking a LUT does not rebuild the composition. A LUT look
  that cannot be drawn (its file missing or refused) zeroes `lookEnabled` and
  binds the placeholder, so the layer draws ungraded; an adjustment clip whose
  only grade is such a look draws nothing. `prepare` resolves the lattice
  once per layer into `PreparedLayer.look`.
- Budget: a 1080p frame in under 8 ms. Textures for image/text/shape
  layers are LRU-cached (64 entries). A still whose decoded colour space is
  HLG- or PQ-based is drawn into a 16-bit-float premultiplied RGBA bitmap
  of that space, its codes unconverted, and uploaded `rgba16Float` with its
  arm (§11 HD-19 (a)). In an HLG project a gain-map still's HDR rendition
  is drawn into a 16-bit-float premultiplied RGBA extended-linear BT.2020
  bitmap with no tone mapping, encoded to PQ in place and uploaded
  `rgba16Float` with its `.pq(.bt2020)` arm and peak, and every still's
  texture sits under an HLG key of its own (§11 HD-22 (b), (f)). Any other
  still whose decoded image is
  deeper than 8 bits a component is drawn into a 16-bit-float
  premultiplied RGBA sRGB bitmap and uploaded `rgba16Float`; every other
  image, text and shape texture is premultiplied `bgra8Unorm` (§11 PR-6).
