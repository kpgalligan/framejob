# 4.3e Inspector — the Transcript tab

The inspector's fifth tab, fourth in the strip between Document and Assistant (§4.3a; road map
"creator-jobs" TX2a and TX2b; §11 TX-20…TX-27). View ▸ Transcript ⌃⌘T shows the
inspector if it is hidden, picks the tab and makes its view first responder;
`select_inspector_tab {tab: "transcript"}` reaches it too. Its content is view
state: nothing here edits the project, and nothing is saved.

**What it shows.** TX1's timeline transcript (§7.9f, §11 TX-10): every heard
word of the edited container — the open nest when one is open — in timeline
order, in paragraphs, each led by its timecode in the canvas's timecode style
(`ReelTime.format`). A paragraph breaks where the track or the speaker label
changes, where two clips (or nest paths) on one track leave 1.5 s or more
between their words, and inside one clip where the voice cache holds 1.5 s of
source time below the voice threshold between two words — with no voice cache,
where 1.5 s of source time lies between them (§11 TX-22). Words that cannot be
cut (§11 TX-11: inside a nest, under a transition, on a locked track) are
faint, and hovering one shows its sentence.

**The bar** (§11 TX-24) is pinned above the text and does not scroll with it:
the pane's one header, hosted by the inspector above its scroll view as the
Clip tab's header is. Two rows, each a label at the inspector's label width
and a popup spanning the rest: **Words from** and **Language**. Under them the
status row, its sentence wrapping, with **Stop** at its right while the tab
reads on this Mac, or **Read** once stopped. Then the wrapping notes: the
same-speech notes (from the words shown), then at most three "clip N —
reason" rows for clips with no words, then "…and N more clips with no
words.", whose tooltip lists the rest. What is empty collapses.

**Words from** (§11 TX-25) lists "All tracks", then each track of the edited
container by name, in the project's order; one choice. With a track chosen
the tab shows only the words on that track — its tokens and their ids are
exactly `get_timeline_transcript {tracks: [id]}`'s — and reads on this Mac
only the media with a clip on that track. A chosen track that leaves the
container falls back to All tracks. The clips with no words are listed for
every track, as the tool lists them.

**Language** (§11 TX-25) lists "Automatic (<the Mac's default>)" — its identifier, en-US —, then every
recognition language installed on this Mac, by name, read once each time the
tab is shown; with none installed it is dimmed. A choice drops the words read
on this Mac and reads them again in that language, each media's run kept
beside the others (`<media>-<language>.json`); a cloud transcript still wins
(TR-11). Both choices are the window's view state: kept while the window is
open, whether the tab shows or not, and never saved.

**Following the playhead.** The highlight is the latest word whose start is at
or before the playhead, an accent-tinted rect behind it, so a word stays lit
through the pause after it. While the transport plays, the view keeps it in
sight unless the person scrolled within the last 3 s.

**Seeking.** A click on a word moves the playhead to its frame-snapped start;
a click on a paragraph's timecode, to the paragraph's start. Playback goes on
if it was playing. A click on a clip in the timeline or on the canvas while
the tab shows keeps the tab and scrolls the clip's first word to the top,
with no seek (§11 TX-20); a selection changed any other way leaves it too.

**Keys** (§11 TX-21), with the view first responder: ← and → select the word
before or after and seek to it; ↑ and ↓ the word nearest in x on the line
above or below; ⇧ with any of these extends the selection without seeking;
⌘A selects every word; Esc clears the selection, and with none reaches the
editor. ⌫, ⌦ and Return reach the editor unchanged (cuts and restore are later
phases). Every other key — Space, J, K, L, I, O, M — works as on the timeline:
the view is not a text view.

**Reading on this Mac.** While the tab shows, every heard media Words from
covers with neither a cloud transcript (TR-11) nor a stored whole-file run is
read on this Mac (§11 TX-12) in the Language chosen (Automatic: the Mac's
default), ONE media at a time, the one under the playhead first, and each
media's words appear as soon as its run is kept. The status reads "Reading
“name” on this Mac… NN % (k of n)"; with no speech model installed, "This Mac
has no speech model installed. Choose File ▸ Generate Captions…, pick a
language and press Download."; a run that fails shows its sentence and is not
tried again until the tab is next shown. **Stop** (§11 TX-26) withdraws only
the tab's own want: the run is cancelled when nobody else wants it, but a
tool call waiting on the same media (`get_timeline_transcript`) keeps it
going. The status then reads "Stopped. N files not read on this Mac." with
Read; Stop holds through edits and through Words from and Language until
Read, or until the tab is next shown. Leaving the tab withdraws its want too.
Nothing is read at import, and nothing here sends anything anywhere (§11
CJ-3).

**Rebuilds.** The text is rebuilt on each new edited project while the tab
shows, never during a live drag (once after it), and the scroll stays on the
word that was at the top (by identity, else the nearest later start).

**Its size.** The pane is exactly the inspector's visible height under the
bar, and the text scrolls in a view of its own, as the Assistant tab's
transcript does (§4.3c); the tab gets the inspector's 280–440 pt.

**For a proof.** The dev tool `transcript_view` (§7.9c) answers what the tab
and its bar show, and can set Words from and Language, press Read, press Stop
a given time after its answer, focus the view, select words and click one.
