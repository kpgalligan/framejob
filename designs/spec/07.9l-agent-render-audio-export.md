# 7.9l Agent — the app-state tools, continued: render_frame, the audio tools and export

Continues §7.9e's run of app-state tools with `render_frame`, `normalize_clip`, `enhance_voice`,
`match_audio`, `sync_audio`, `export`, `export_status` and `cancel_export`, moved here whole
because §7.9e had passed the spec's ~500-line split rule (#585, 2026-11-11). They were §7.9e's
until then, so an older citation of §7.9e for one of them means this file. Scope, documents,
media, captions, detection and the transcript tools are §7.9e.

`render_frame` is ask-first for the assistant only with `path`, §7.9b.
`render_frame {t, max_width?, path?, format?, burn_in_timecode?, ungraded?,
sdr_version?, selection?, waveform?, parade?, vectorscope?, vectorscope_zoom?, histogram?}` → a PNG,
JPEG or HEIC (the verification hook: the compositor renders offline through
an `AVAssetImageGenerator` over the same composition + video composition,
or, for a PNG or HEIC of a frame showing a graphic, or a still or a VP9 track
deeper than 8 bits, or one the generator answers 8-bit while it shows a deeper
source, export's `AVAssetReader` at 16-bit, §11 PR-9, PR-12, PR-14, PR-20).
With no `path` and no `format` it writes a `.png` to a temporary file and
ends its text `Written to <path>.` exactly as it always has, which five
proof scripts read the path back out of; an explicit `format` wins over the
path's own extension and the extension is REPLACED rather than appended to.
The write is `FrameWriter`'s — the one function File ▸ Save Frame… calls
too, which is what makes those two files byte-identical by construction
rather than by luck, and the only way the line can be checked at all, since
no script can press a save panel. `burn_in_timecode` is the recipe option of
§7.3 and not an export override, and this tool is the only one that can
photograph a burn-in. `ungraded` is the same kind of option (§11 GR-4: every
`video.color` neutral, every wheel neutral, GR-11, every curve the identity, GR-12, and every look removed, GR-7, an adjustment clip's
included; ", with every grade removed" added to the first
sentence) and View ▸ Show Ungraded's mirror, since the switch itself never
reaches this tool; `sdr_version` (hdr H-3, §11 HD-7, HD-14) is the same
kind again and View ▸ Show SDR Version's mirror: an HLG project's SDR version,
the first sentence gaining ", as the project's SDR version"; a JPEG of an HLG
project is drawn so without it, and an SDR project's frame is unchanged by
it, byte for byte; `selection` (§11 GR-13) is an adjustment clip's id, the
recipe option of §7.3 and View ▸ Show Selection's mirror: that clip's
qualifier drawn as grey (white fully graded, black untouched, blurred when its `blur` is above 0, times any matte
cutting it), every layer in front of it left out, black where the clip is not
on the frame; an id that names no adjustment clip of the edited project is
refused naming `selection`, and the first sentence gains ", drawn as clip N's
qualifier selection (white fully graded, black untouched)"; `waveform`, `parade`, `vectorscope` and `histogram` are
View ▸ Scopes' four scopes (§11 GR-5, GR-10): one analysis of the frame
through the panel's own analyser for every one asked, each written beside the
image as `<path>.<scope>.json` and said in one sentence of its own, in that
order, before the last. `waveform`: `columns`, `levels`, `width`, `height`,
`counts` (256 arrays by column bin, each indexed by level), `clipped_high`,
`clipped_low`, `marks_high`, `marks_low`, `highlights`, `shadows`; `Waveform:
highlights clipped in X % of pixels, shadows in Y %; its 256 × 256 counts are in
<json path>.` `parade`: `columns`, `levels`, `width`, `height`, and `counts`,
`marks_high`, `marks_low`, `clipped_high` and `clipped_low` each keyed `red`,
`green` and `blue` (the counts 256 arrays by column bin, indexed by level);
`Parade: red clips high in X % of pixels and low in Y %, green in X % and Y %,
blue in X % and Y %; its 3 × 256 × 256 counts are in <json path>.`
`vectorscope`: `bins` (256), `centre` (128), `width`, `height`, `matrix`
("Rec. 709"), `values` ("encoded"), `counts` (256 arrays by Cr bin, each
indexed by Cb bin), `neutral` (the pixels in bin (128, 128)), `zoom` (1, 2 or
4), `off_plot` (the pixels not counted, off the plot at a zoom; 0 at 1),
`targets` (`R`, `Mg`, `B`, `Cy`, `G`, `Yl`, each [Cb bin, Cr bin] at the zoom,
`null` when off the plot) and `skin_line_degrees`; `Vectorscope (Rec. 709 Cb
and Cr of the encoded values): N % of pixels are neutral; its 256 × 256 counts
are in <json path>.` `vectorscope_zoom` (§11 GR-31; 1, 2 or 4, default 1) is
a vectorscope slot's zoom cells' mirror: the counts binned `128 +
round(255·zoom·c)`, a pixel off the plot at 2 and 4 not counted; zoomed, the
sentence is `Vectorscope at ×4 (Rec. 709 Cb and Cr of the encoded values): N %
of pixels are neutral and M % lie off the plot; its 256 × 256 counts are in
<json path>.` Before the render, any other value is refused as
`vectorscope_zoom must be 1, 2 or 4 (got 3).`, a non-integer in the argument
reader's own sentence, and the zoom without `vectorscope: true` as
`vectorscope_zoom needs vectorscope: true.` `histogram`: `levels`,
`width`, `height`, `red`, `green`, `blue`, `luma` (each 256 counts),
`clipped_high`, `clipped_low`, `highlights`, `shadows` (by Y′); `Histogram:
highlights clipped in X % of pixels, shadows in Y % (by Y′); its R′, G′, B′ and
Y′ counts per 256 levels are in <json path>.` **In an HLG project** (§11 HD-8,
HD-15) the scopes read the frame drawn: its own picture as the HLG signal, on
BT.2020's weights and matrix, the waveform's, parade's and histogram's JSON
adding `signal` ("hlg") and `scale` (`unit` "cd/m²", `ticks` — `percent`,
`level`, `nits` at 0/0/0.0, 25/64/9.6, 50/128/50.7, 75/191/203.2 and
100/255/1000.0 — `reference_white_percent` 75 and `clip_percent` 100), the
vectorscope's `signal`, `matrix` "Rec. 2020" and BT.2020's `targets` and
`skin_line_degrees`; the sentences become `Waveform (HLG signal; reference
white at 75 %, 203 cd/m², and 100 % at 1,000 cd/m²): …`, `Parade (HLG
signal): …`, `Vectorscope (Rec. 2020 Cb and Cr of the HLG values): …` (zoomed
`Vectorscope at ×4 (Rec. 2020 …): …`) and `Histogram (HLG signal): … (by Y′,
BT.2020 weights); …`. `sdr_version` and a JPEG frame read the SDR version as
an SDR project's frame is read: no `signal` or `scale` key, the sentences as
above. Neither the switch nor the panel
nor its slot picks and zooms has a tool of its own (§7.9's Preview Quality exemption). When a graphic in the frame warned or failed, one
sentence per report precedes that last one — `Graphic "x" failed at frame
N: <message> (line L, column C).`, `… warned at frame N: …` or `… does not
compile: …` — and a failed graphic draws its error card in the picture
(road map 10's G3c); the call is still not an error, and

`sync_audio {clips, anchor?, accept_weak?}` (road maps "audio-sync" AS-1 and AS-2 and
"audio-sync-2" AS-3 and AS-4 and its round 3, spec §11 SY-1..SY-22 and
SY-28..SY-30 — the mirror of Clip ▸
Synchronize Audio; a composite, not an op: one anchor stays and every other
clip moves by a frame-snapped `move_clip`, matched on the cached sync
features of the clips' played windows first and the whole files only when
that chooses nothing — their speech frames only when both stretches hold
at least 2 s of speech by the cached Silero VAD (SY-17) — its clock drift corrected by a `set_speed {rate,
keep_pitch: false}` when the drift is at least max(5 ms, 3 × its
uncertainty) and the new speed stays within 0.2% of ×1, and a mover that
would start before 0 trimmed at its head by a `trim_start` so it starts at
0 (SY-16), all one undo step, or the call is refused naming every reason
and nothing moves — a match not chosen names each search's candidates as
the move each would make. A ramped clip, mover or anchor, is refused (§11
SR-20 (e)). A match is CONFIDENT at z ≥ 10 and 1.5 above the
next candidate and applies; one chosen only near where the mover sits (z ≥
4 within 3 s) is WEAK (SY-18) and, when it would move anything, is NOT
applied unless `accept_weak` is true — the Weak match sheet's Apply
(SY-19): the answer is `{ok: true, moved: false, anchor, synced, weak,
note, project}`, no op and no undo step, and an agent passes `accept_weak:
true` only after the person has agreed to that move. Each match's offset is
refined below a frame by band-limited GCC-PHAT on the files' cached 8 kHz
samples within ±48 ms of the match (SY-20), over the played overlap, or the
files' overlap at the match when that is under 2 s; EITHER tier stands only
on a clear peak there (z ≥ 15, SY-29): a match whose waveforms do not line up

…), so they are not one recording.", and one whose readings hold samples
but whose refinement cannot run is refused "…but they share too little sound
there to confirm it is one recording…" (fail closed). Drift is measured on
the features, each window then refined by PHAT (SY-21). It runs off the main thread (SY-22): a project or scope changed while
it matched is the error "the project changed while Synchronize Audio was
matching, so nothing moved. Call sync_audio again.", and the call still
blocks until it answers. It answers `moved`,
`anchor`, `weak` (the clip ids of its weak moves, [] when none) and per mover

`coarse_offset` the match before it, `refined` "phat" or "coarse",

"features" or "phat" and `drift_windows` the windows fitted (both null when

`matched_on` "speech" or "sound", `trimmed` the ticks
cut off the mover's head (0 when none) and `start` its final start — with
`moved: false` and "already in sync" when no op was needed; no `scope`), and
`export {preset, path, size?, range?, video_bitrate?, audio_bitrate?,
loudness_lufs?, peak_dbtp?, burn_in_timecode?, dynamic_range?, encoder_speed?, audio_bits?, stems?}` — the export sheet's
controls in the sheet's own vocabulary (all but the frame rate: the tool
exports at the document's, or on GIF and APNG at the format's cap), so an
agent export and a menu export can ask for the same file. **It answers at
once** (§11 #326): every refusal — an argument, the core's settings, a
missing folder, a graphic clip whose module does not compile, a GIF or APNG
range over 600 frames, an alpha row at a size of another shape (§11 #470) —
comes back from the call itself, before anything is queued; then the export is a job
in the Export Queue, exactly as the sheet's Add to Queue makes one (the
window is not opened, and one job runs at a time, so it may wait behind a
person's), and the answer is `{export_id, path, status}`, `status`
`queued` or `running`. `export_status {export_id}` follows it and never
waits: always `export_id`, `status` (`queued`, `running`, `done`, `failed`
or `cancelled`), `path` and `elapsed` (seconds from the `export` call to
now, or to the finish); `running` adds `progress` (0…1) and `phase` when
the job has one: `"Preparing…"` until the file's writer exists (renders,
masks and measurements still being made) and `"Measuring loudness…"`
during the loudness pass; `queued` adds `waiting`, the queue row's sentence,
while the memory limit holds an `av1`, `av1_webm`, `vp9_webm` or
`vp9_alpha_webm` job behind
the app's other encoder exports (§11 CX-18), and `queued` and `running` add
`estimated_seconds` on those rows, the export sheet's estimate of the whole
write in whole seconds; `failed` and `cancelled` add `reason`, the queue
row's sentence; and `done` adds `message`, the queue row's sentence too
("Exported “name”", then " and N stems" when stems were written, then any
warning and the loudness figures; §11 #395), and what `export` answered when
it blocked to the file. `cancel_export {export_id}` is the queue row's Cancel for a queued
or running job (a running one may still read `running` until the writer
stops) and leaves a finished one alone, answering as `export_status` does.
Both refuse an id no `export` returned this launch by name; the records
last until the app quits and outlive the row's Remove and Clear Finished.
The done answer carries what the file's sound MEASURED — `loudness`:
`summary`, `integrated_lufs`, `true_peak_dbtp`, `sample_peak_dbfs`, the
`gain_db` that was applied, `ceiling_dbtp` (the master limiter's, §11
AU-27), `limited_db` (how far the master took the hottest peak down),
`capped_by_gain` (the +40 dB cap decided the gain, AU-28) and `target_lufs`
when one was asked; no `capped_by_peak` — for every export that has any, asked for or
not, which is `detect_silence`'s posture applied to the writer and is what
makes the ±0.5 LU claim checkable over MCP. A finished export also answers
`graphic_warnings [{name, frame, message}]`: each distinct warning a graphic
gave while the file was drawn, once per graphic and message, with the first
frame (the graphic's own) it was given at; the key is absent when there was
none (spec §11 G-24 (c)). So is `cursor_warnings [{media, path, message}]`:
each media whose cursor file could not be drawn, its clips drawn without one
(§11 CU-37 (c)); `render_frame` draws a clip's cursor (CU-33) and adds "The
cursor of media M is not drawn: <message>." per such file before its last
sentence. A clip with a cutout waits up to 30 s for the masks the frame
draws (a picture's of the kind its subject asks, §11 CO-106) (§11 CO-22 (f), `Exporter.frameCutoutWait`; File ▸ Save Frame… waits the
same); past that `render_frame` draws it uncut and adds "Clip N is drawn
uncut: its person masks were not ready within 30 s." (a foreground picture's
"its foreground masks") per such clip, ascending,
before its last sentence. `dynamic_range` (hdr H-3, §11 HD-14 (c)) is the
sheet's Dynamic Range popup, forwarded to the core unread (§7.8 has its
per-row defaults and both refusals), and the result names the picture
written as `dynamic_range` in an HLG project. `encoder_speed` (codec-export
C1, §11 CX-12 (2)) is the sheet's Speed popup — `faster`, `balanced` or
`smaller` on `av1` and `av1_webm` (C3, CX-15) — forwarded unread the same way
(the core refuses a word that is not one of them and a speed on any other
row, `vp9_webm` and `vp9_alpha_webm` among them: libvpx has one speed, C4, CX-16, CX-17), and the done answer names the speed the file was encoded at as
`encoder_speed`. `audio_bits` (road map "audio" AP2 part 2, §11 AU-29) is
the sheet's Bit depth popup — 16 or 24 on `audio_wav` and `audio_aiff` —
forwarded unread the same way (the core refuses another value and a depth on
any other row), and the done answer names the depth on those two rows as
`audio_bits`. `stems` (road map "audio" AP10 part 2a, §11 AU-108) is the
sheet's Stems popup — `tracks` or `names` on `audio_m4a`, `audio_wav` and
`audio_aiff` — forwarded unread the same way (the core refuses another word,
a movie row and a project with no track heard), and the done answer lists
each file beside the mix as `stems [{name, tracks, path}]`; a stem path that
is one of the project's media fails the job with `reason` naming the file
(§7.8). `peak_dbtp` is the master limiter's ceiling, −1 when absent,
legal with or without `loudness_lufs` (AU-27). `audio_bitrate` is the AAC or Opus rate.
`burn_in_timecode` is the one argument of the ten that is not a setting:
it is a recipe option (§7.3), travelling beside the resolved settings rather
than inside them, because the override table ignores a key it does not
know — an option posted that way would vanish without a word and the tool
would report success on an un-burnt file. The done answer echoes it back
for the same reason.
